# Komorebi — A Living Specification

*Sunlight filtering through the leaves of trees, and the shapes it casts on the ground.*

---

## 0. What this document is

This is a vision-and-specification document for an HTML5 / WebGL visualization of **komorebi** (木漏れ日, literally "sunlight that leaks through the trees"). It is meant to be a **living spec**: a single source of truth that another implementer — human or AI — can build from, return to, and revise as the work develops.

It holds three things, deliberately kept distinct:

1. **The vision and lived experience** that started this — the north star. When a later implementation decision is ambiguous, this is the arbiter. The point is not to reproduce a reference image; it is to recreate something that was *felt*.
2. **The physics** of the phenomenon, worked out from first principles.
3. **The computational model** — how to render it faithfully, and *why* it is built this way.

The governing intent, stated once so it colors everything below: **model the reality, not the artifacts.** The look should *emerge* from correct physics rather than being painted on. Where we approximate, we say so explicitly and we say why it is safe.

## 1. The lived experience (the north star)

This began with an afternoon under the trees in a park, in **early spring** — the leaves not yet fully grown, the canopy still sparse.

On the ground were the familiar dapples of light. But looked at closely, they were not crisp. They were **soft, out of focus** — like looking through an old prime lens, the kind with character. And the focus was not fixed: thick and thin clouds were drifting across the sun, and as they passed, the whole pattern **breathed in and out of focus**, blooming soft and then sharpening again. The bokeh on the ground was the thing — that very out-of-focus, glowing, low-contrast quality.

Then the wind, and two motions remembered vividly:

- **A strong directional breeze** that pushed all the leaves to one side, so the gaps on the ground **translated almost perfectly** — sliding sideways while keeping their shapes. You could *feel the breathing of the wind*, an easing-in rather than a switch flipping. And because branches can only move so far, at the peak of a gust you could feel the **elasticity of the branches fighting the wind** — and then, when the wind died, they returned to their original starting point. There is also a **backlash at that stiffening**: bent hard against their limit, the branches recoil — they snap back past rest and settle, not glide home.

- **A very faint wind, in all directions at once**, the kind that seems to come up when the sun shines a little stronger. This did not translate anything. It made the leaves **rearrange**, opening entirely new gaps. Because it was early spring and the foliage was sparse, *individual leaves still mattered* — moving one of them genuinely changed a gap — so the pattern reorganized into something wholly new. It looked, unmistakably, like **water caustics moving across the floor of a pool as waves collapse**: bright shapes merging, splitting, and dissolving rather than sliding.

And under the very faintest breeze, the shapes seemed to **glisten** — a fine twinkling in place.

Everything that follows is in service of that afternoon.

**And beneath the optics, the afternoon carried a feeling the software must carry too: calm.** The mindfulness of lying under the trees — peace, an innate selfness, the oneness with nature. Anyone in front of this software should feel that. It is an arbiter beside the physics: the piece never startles, never performs, never asks for attention — it is a place a person settles into, not a thing they operate. Where a decision passes every technical test but disturbs the stillness, the stillness wins.

## 2. Guiding philosophy

Four commitments, in priority order:

1. **Model reality, not the artifacts.** Reproduce the *causes* and let the appearance fall out. The sun-images, the soft penumbrae, the translation, the reorganizing, the twinkle should all be *consequences* of a correct model, never special-cased effects.

2. **Never fake the structure, never fake the optics law.** Leaves are really where they are. Light really attenuates through them (Beer's law). The light source really integrates over its finite angular size. These are inviolate.

3. **There is exactly one sanctioned approximation: depth discretization.** We collapse the continuous depth of the canopy into a small number of layers (3–5). This is the *only* place we cheat, and it is the right place: it is where performance genuinely bites, and simultaneously where the eye is most forgiving. The eye needs "some dapples crisp, some soft"; it cannot count the layers. *(One smaller, optional optical approximation later joins it — the leaf-edge **diffraction** of §3.6, which rides this same transport shift rather than integrating a true diffraction kernel. It is off by default, so depth discretization stays the one cheat every frame pays.)* *(And when a look needs the tree **seen as an object** — the standing scene, the bedroom curtain — `faithful_canopy` (§4.5) is the **opt-out from even this cheat**: it casts the actual geometry at real continuous heights, so the shadow *is* the tree. The cheat stays the **default** — the overhead park keeps it and pays nothing — but it is no longer the only path where fidelity to the tree's structure is what reaches the surface.)*

4. **Only model what reaches the surface.** We never *see* the tree — only the light it throws on the ground, viewed from below. So the canopy must be faithful only in the ways that affect the cast pattern: where leaves are, their height, size, orientation, and how they move together. Anything about how the tree looks from the side that does not change the cast pattern, we decline to simulate. This is not faking; it is refusing to model the irrelevant, and it is what keeps the whole thing light. *(Amended as the project widens past the park: when a look is **backlit and seen from the side** — sunlight through a tree onto a bedroom curtain, the scene we are building toward — the tree's own **trunk and branches** are part of what reaches the surface, as a dark silhouette, not just its leaf-gaps. So the woody skeleton joins the leaves as an occluder, gated by `branch_tau` (§4.5) and **off by default** — the overhead looks that never see the trunk still pay nothing and render byte-identically. The principle is unchanged — model only what reaches the surface — but "the surface" is no longer always the floor, so "what reaches it" can now include the tree itself.)* *(**Amended a second time, and further** (`sky_view`, §4.9): lying in a hammock looking **up**, the surface the light reaches is the **retina** — so the tree is not merely part of what reaches a surface, it is the thing in frame. Trunks converge overhead, crowns glow backlit, the source itself shows through the gaps. Even here the principle survives intact, because nothing about the tree is *drawn*: the same occluder field is read along the eye's rays instead of the sun's, and what you see is still only transmission. Off by default; the overhead looks are untouched.)* *(**Amended a third time, and this one reverses it** (§4.9's sky view again): in the canopy looks **we should see the tree.** Not its cast, not its silhouette as a by-product of transmission — the tree, as an object. What reaches the eye there has to read as one: **dark connected wood** running trunk → limb → twig → leaf without a gap, thinning by the pipe model as it goes, and **occlusion that is never luminous** — an occluder that outshines what it occludes stops being an object and becomes a light. The principle it replaces was a budget rule, and it earned its keep for as long as the ground was the only surface; it stops applying the moment the tree is the subject. What this does **not** yet include is named so the direction is legible rather than implied: there is no lit bark, no sun on a trunk's near flank, no visible-surface shading of any kind. The tree here is still an object made of shadow — correct in its structure and its connectivity, and unlit. **The tree as a shaded body is the direction**, and it is not built.)*

## 3. The optics

### 3.1 The master fact: the sun is not a point

The sun has an angular diameter of roughly **half a degree** — about **1/100 of a radian**. Every soft thing in komorebi descends from this single fact. A point source would give razor-sharp shadows; an extended source cannot.

### 3.2 The dapples are images of the sun

The most important and least obvious truth: **most of the bright shapes on the ground are not the shapes of the gaps between the leaves. They are images of the sun.**

Each small gap in the canopy acts as a **pinhole camera**. If a gap is small enough relative to its height above the ground, it stops projecting *its own shape* and instead projects a picture of the light source behind it — and that source is the sun's disk. So the soft roundish dapples are little suns, hundreds of them, overlapping.

The proof is the partial solar eclipse: when the sun becomes a crescent, **every dapple on the ground becomes a crescent too.** The shapes track the source, not the holes.

This gives **two regimes**, governed by gap size `a` versus the blur scale `h·θ` (height × the sun's angular size):

- **Pinhole regime** (`a ≪ h·θ`): the gap's own shape washes out; you see a clean **image of the sun**.
- **Shadow regime** (`a ≫ h·θ`): you see a bright patch shaped like **the gap**, bordered by a fuzzy penumbra of width `~h·θ`.

For a canopy at ~10 m, `h·θ ≈ 10 cm` — which is why typical dapples are fist- to plate-sized and inescapably soft.

### 3.3 Blurriness is the *source*, not focus

The clean way to see this — and it maps directly onto the renderer — is a **convolution**:

> ground illuminance = (sharp gap map) ⊛ (the source's angular brightness profile, scaled to the ground by height)

The blur kernel *is* the source. Both regimes above fall out of this one operation automatically.

A critical consequence for implementers: **there is no focal plane here, and no plane of best focus. A pinhole has no focus.** The "out of focus" quality is *not* a focal-distance effect. Sharpness is set entirely by how large the source looks and how large the gap is. Do not model a focal plane.

### 3.4 Clouds turn the blur knob

On a clear day the source is the sun's bare ~0.5° disk: a crisp kernel, dapples as sharp as the canopy allows. When **thin cloud** drifts across, its droplets scatter the sunlight and smear the source into a bright **aureole** many degrees wide. The kernel fattens; every dapple bloats and softens. **Thick cloud** takes this to the limit — the source grows to fill the sky, the kernel becomes enormous, and the pattern dissolves into flat shade.

So the focus *breathing in and out* under drifting cloud is the **size of the light source changing over time** — nothing more. This is the single most expressive parameter in the whole piece.

Two felt qualities come *for free* from the same mechanism, which is why it read as an old prime lens:

- **Contrast sag.** As the source widens, each dapple's light spreads over a larger spot (peak dims) while the dark gaps fill with scattered light (shadows lift). Sharpness and contrast drop *together* — exactly the veiled, glowing look of old uncoated glass.
- **A bokeh duality worth keeping in mind.** In a camera, an out-of-focus point of light renders the shape of the *aperture* (why bokeh balls take the iris shape). In komorebi it is the inverse: a small gap renders the shape of the *source*. Camera bokeh shows you the hole; komorebi shows you the sun. Cloud, by inflating the source, pushes komorebi harder into that "you are seeing the source" regime.

### 3.5 Two realism details

- **The dapples are elliptical, not circular**, because the ground is tilted relative to the sun. The lower the sun, the more they stretch — elongated along the sun's direction, sheared toward its azimuth.
- **The "dark" areas are not black; they are a dim green.** That light passed *through* thin leaves, and chlorophyll transmits green while absorbing red and blue. The real scene is bright white-gold suns floating on a soft green-grey wash — never white-on-black.
- **The sunlight itself reddens as the sun lowers — and the shade goes blue.** At low elevation the beam crosses far more atmosphere (air mass by Kasten–Young, *not* `1/sin` — that diverges at the horizon); Rayleigh scattering (∝ λ⁻⁴) strips its blue/green, so the disk warms white → gold → orange → red. Meanwhile the ozone **Chappuis band** absorbs red from the *scattered* light, so the skylight that fills the shadows stays **blue** (the "blue hour") — which is why a low sun reads as *warm light on cool shade*. Both are modelled from `sun_elevation` by a cheap 3-band (R/G/B) Beer's-law atmosphere (`uSunColor` = the beam, `uAmbient` = the sky), with a `sky_turbidity` haze knob; the green wash above is the *transmitted* beam, the blue is the *skylight* — distinct mechanisms that compose. Computed in linear HDR, upstream of exposure and tone-map.
- **At dusk the eye itself cools the shade — the Purkinje shift.** As the light fails the retina's rods take over from the cones (mesopic vision): rods are colour-blind and peak in the blue-green (~505 nm), so the dim areas **desaturate toward a blue-grey** and **saturated reds darken first**, while anything still bright (the dapples) stays in cone vision, warm and coloured. It is a fact of *perception*, not of the light — but it is real and modelable, so it earns its place over a hand-grade. komorebi has no absolute luminance, so it is driven by two *honest* cues: a global "duskness" ramped from `sun_elevation` (a low sun is dim) **times** the *local* shade darkness (`acc`, the exposure-independent transmittance). Only the deep shade between the suns goes cool; the suns themselves are untouched. A `mesopic_strength` knob sets how far, and it hard-gates off for a daytime sun — the perception-honest route to the journal's "twilight is cool and soft," modelled rather than painted.

### 3.6 The colours split at the edges — leaf-edge diffraction

A later observation, brought in from looking at glass: through some lenses the bright and dark edges fringe, the red and blue of a highlight **splitting apart**. That split has an honest home here. The dapples are pinhole images formed at the gaps between leaves, and at every **leaf edge** the light **diffracts** — bends around the obstruction by an angle that grows with wavelength, **θ ∝ λ**. Red, the longest visible wavelength, spreads the most; blue the least. So the red image of the sun is thrown a touch *wider* than the blue one, and the two no longer land exactly on top of each other.

The consequence on the ground: **every dapple gains a warm outer rim and a cool core**, and the soft penumbra between a sun-image and the shade breaks into a faint spectrum. This is the felt "split" — but it is a *consequence of a real cause* (diffraction at the canopy's countless edges), never a prism painted over the picture. The leaves really do this; we are only making it visible.

It composes with everything already here, for free. The split is a per-wavelength change in the **angular** offset of the source, so it rides the very same **height × angle** shift that carries light to the ground (§4.6): higher foliage fringes more (its images are thrown further, so the wavelengths separate further), and a low sun — whose ellipse already stretches the dapples — stretches the fringe along with them. The three wavelengths are the same R/G/B bands (620 / 555 / 470 nm) the atmosphere model uses (§3.5), so the spread ratio is physically grounded rather than invented.

**The one honesty.** True diffraction would integrate a wavelength-dependent kernel at each gap, sized by the gap. We instead **scale the geometric pinhole shift per channel** by `λ/λ_green` — a cheap proxy that couples the dispersion to the *geometric* throw rather than to the gap width. It is a **second, smaller sanctioned approximation** alongside the depth discretization of §2, and it earns its place the same way: a real mechanism, off by default (strength 0 collapses the three channels back to one shared tap, byte-identical), and an expressive knob honestly *exaggerated* above the true ~λ-proportional value, the way the Purkinje shift of §3.5 is real-but-amplified. At realistic strength it is a hairline; pushed up, it is the **prism** look.

## 4. The computational model

### 4.1 It is all one thing: soft shadows from an area light

Everything above — pinhole sun-images, penumbra softness, the breathing cloud focus — is a single well-understood problem in costume: **soft shadows cast by an area light**, where the area light is exactly the source profile of §3.4 (sun disk plus variable cloud halo). A point light gives hard shadows; an area light gives penumbrae; a larger, softer light gives softer shadows. The cloud widening the source *is* the light growing.

### 4.2 The method: accumulate many small lights ("many suns")

The faithful, and barely harder, way to compute the convolution is to **sample the source and accumulate**, rather than blurring a finished picture:

> Scatter N weighted point-suns across the source. Each casts a perfectly **sharp** shadow. A sun offset by a small angle simply **shifts** each occluder by (its height × that angle). **Sum the N shifted sharp shadows**, and the sum *is* the soft shadow — the convolution, computed by sampling the light.

This was also a direct observation in the park: the big soft light is *literally an accumulation of many smaller ones*. The method matches the perception.

The payoff is the whole game: because the shift scales with height, **occluders at different depths blur by different amounts, for free.** And the cloud knob becomes literal — cloud thickness is just how widely the samples spread.

**Alternatives considered (and why not):**

- *Single global blur* — render one flat gap-map, blur once. Produces sun-images and penumbrae, but gives **every** dapple the same softness; it cannot vary blur with depth, so it reads as "a photo with a Gaussian blur," not light through a tree. **Rejected.**
- *Accumulation / many-suns* — **chosen.** Faithful, gives depth-varying softness, wires cloud straight into the light.
- *Per-pixel ray sampling from the ground up* — the same computation, more general but noisier. Not the starting point.

### 4.3 The architecture: four separable subsystems

The whole renderer is four parts, kept independent so that *reality* and *art* can be tuned without fighting:

| Subsystem | Role | Moves? |
|---|---|---|
| **Source** | the area light — a cloud of weighted point-suns | no (changes only with cloud) |
| **Canopy** | geometry only — fields of leaf occluders | **yes — the only thing that moves** |
| **Transport** | the shift-multiply-sum that carries light to the ground | no (pure physics) |
| **Look** | projection, color, exposure, tone-mapping | no |

### 4.4 The Source

The source is **not "a light." It is a cloud of weighted point-suns** — a list of `(angular offset, weight)` pairs.

- **Sampling pattern: a Vogel / sunflower (Fibonacci-angle) spiral.** Even, deterministic disk coverage with no clumping and no banding. ~**16–48 samples** holds 60 fps in a fragment shader.
- **The samples must be frame-stable.** If they jitter every frame they produce a numerical shimmer — and we must **not** let sampling noise impersonate the physical glisten. *All* visible motion comes from the canopy; none from the math.
- **Two populations**, matching the physics: a tight, heavy **core** (the ~0.5° sun, most of the energy) and a sparse, wide, low-weight **halo** (the cloud aureole).
- **The cloud knob** shifts weight from core to halo and spreads the halo points wider. Peak dims, total light conserved, contrast sags — the breathing of §3.4.
- **Keep the source purely angular and circular.** The elliptical ground stretch does **not** belong here; it lives in transport.

### 4.5 The Canopy

**The branch hierarchy is the truth.** It is a real 3D moving structure where the leaves live and where motion happens. The layered textures we render are merely a *per-frame snapshot* of that truth, in a form the light step can read fast. You do not choose between "a real tree" and "layers" — you have a real moving structure, and each frame you bake its current state into layers.

**A leaf, as data**, is tiny: which layer it is in, a **rest position** in the canopy plane (plan view, looking straight down), a size, an orientation, an **RGB transmittance**, and (for motion) spring state. The canopy never knows where the sun is — that keeps the separation honest.

**Gaps and clumps are emergent, never authored.** This is the correction that motion forces: because gaps *change when leaves move*, gaps cannot be the thing you draw — they are leftovers, the negative space between leaves. So:

- Grow a **light branch skeleton** (two or three levels: a few limbs → twigs → leaf clusters) and hang leaves on it.
- **Clumping is just the topology** — leaves cluster on twigs, twigs on branches, automatically.
- **Multi-scale gaps come from the skeleton's self-similarity** — big gaps between branches (shape-patches) and tiny gaps between leaves (sun-images), for free.
- The **gap-size distribution is a tuning target**, not a thing you generate directly.

**Now realized — a grown grove.** This is no longer a stand-in. The canopy is generated by growing several small recursive trees — a *grove* — each a trunk → a few limbs → `branch_children` sub-branches recursing `branch_levels` deep → terminal twigs that carry the leaf clusters. The trees' **crowns overlap to fill the frame**: one tree grown from a single central trunk leaves a bare-bright hole over its trunk (all foliage hangs outboard in a ring), so a grove is used instead — which also matches the park, several trees, the smaller ones reading denser (the same cluster packed into a smaller crown). Leaves **bin to depth layers by the height they grew to**, so higher foliage blurs more for free, and **multi-scale gaps fall out of the branching self-similarity** as intended. The fill radius is tied to `view_extent` so the grove fills the frame at any zoom. Knobs: `tree_count`, `limb_count` (arms per tree), `branch_levels`, `branch_children`, `branch_angle`, `branch_length_ratio`, `branch_pitch`. `tree_count` is treated as a **continuous** quantity — a fractional value renders `floor` full trees plus a marginal tree faded in by the remainder, and trees are appended at the end of the growth RNG stream so a fractional count only adds/removes the *last* tree — so a tree-count change can **morph** (the grove grows a tree in, the rest re-spacing to make room) rather than pop (§9), while integer counts render exactly as before. *(The earlier clustered-point scatter is retired; `clusters_per_layer` survives only as a legacy preset key.)*

**The bake — "becomes a texture":**

- Once per frame, rasterize current leaf positions into **3–5 depth-binned layers**.
- **Stamp optical depth, not transmittance.** Transmittances *multiply* (light must clear every leaf), which forces sorting of overlaps. Optical depth (`−log transmittance`) *adds*, so leaves accumulate with plain **additive blending — order-independent, no sorting.** Overlaps darken correctly on their own. Transport reads transmittance back as `exp(−depth)`.
- Because depth is **per-channel** (high red/blue, low green for a leaf), two overlapping leaves land twice as deep — darker *and* greener. **The green shadow wash falls straight out of Beer's law** instead of being painted on.
- **Soft edges come for free and honestly**: a leaf edge crossing a texel only partly covers it, so that texel gets a fraction of the depth. Area-averaging at finite resolution.
- **Orientation-driven footprint (bake this in from the start).** A leaf face-on blocks its full area; edge-on it blocks almost nothing. So its stamp is its true shape **foreshortened by tilt**. When a leaf flutters, its footprint shrinks and swells, winking small gaps open and shut — **a real chunk of the glisten lives right here in the stamp, not in transport.** Treat a leaf as shape-plus-orientation projecting to a footprint, never a fixed blob.

**The woody shadow — trunk + main limbs as a CONNECTED analytic occluder (gated, `branch_tau`).** At first only leaves were stamped; the woody skeleton (grown all along for *motion*, §5) wasn't an occluder. A **backlit tree seen from the side** (the bedroom scene) needs it. The first attempt stamped each branch into the leaf **depth-layers** — and it **shattered**: layer quantization snaps a continuous branch's height to a few discrete values, and at a low sun the shadow shift between adjacent layers is ~1 m, so a branch spanning two layers *jumped*, and the (continuously-cast) trunk floated free of the snapped branches — a "snowflake." Leaves are a cloud and don't care; thin continuous wood does. So the woody shadow is now a **continuous-height analytic occluder**: the **trunk + main limbs** (level ≤ 1) are line segments whose two endpoint **heights** map through the *same crown curve the leaves bin by* (and the bole linearly down to 0), so a limb's base meets the trunk at a **shared height** and the whole shadow **connects** — each segment's shadow runs from a's projection to b's, summing into one connected streak. The **fine sub-branches are dropped**; the leaf dapples carry the fine canopy (physically right — thin twigs blend into the dappled mass rather than throwing distinct shadows). It is evaluated **once per pixel** (per-sample stalled the GPU) using the central sun direction (the §4.8 bulk offset), with a soft edge that **grows with height** to fake the area-light penumbra; the limbs **swing with the wind** (the live limb bend rotates each segment about its trunk, so the wood tracks the leaves) and **drift** with the trunk — the drift **height-scaled** (0 at the foot, full at the crown) so the trunk stays **planted** rather than sliding bodily, the same anchoring the faithful wood uses. `branch_tau` is the wood's optical depth (Beer's law); **0 = no wood, byte-identical**, and it's gated like the diffraction toggle (§3.6). This is the "occluders are a **set of producers**" seam — grown leaves, grown wood, later authored architecture like the window grid — feeding the shadow. *(This analytic occluder is the **layer-mode** wood — trunk + main limbs only, the fine twigs dropped; the **faithful** path below bakes the whole skeleton, twigs included, as real geometry instead.)*

**The faithful canopy — casting the real tree (`faithful_canopy`, the opt-out from the one cheat).** The depth-layer bake is invisible and correct for the overhead park — you never see the tree, only the dapple field, so *where a leaf is relative to the trunk* is not even a question. But the moment the tree is **seen as an object** (the standing scene, the curtain), the layer band betrays it: leaves snap to a few flat heights, decoupled from the continuous twig they grew on — what the leaves cast is no longer the tree (the user's "exploding sandwich"). `faithful_canopy` is a **gated opt-out** (off = the layer fast path, park byte-identical): it casts the **actual geometry at real grown heights**, so the shadow *is* the tree in the 3D preview. Mechanically it runs the "many suns" convolution (§4.2) as **geometry** instead of texture taps: for each source sample, every leaf — and the **whole woody skeleton, trunk + branches + the fine twigs** (tapered capsules, no level cap, unlike the analytic occluder) — is drawn at its own continuous height projected to the floor (`world − height·g_sample`) with **multiplicative** blend (transmittance = Π e^−τ), and the per-sample buffers are weight-summed into **one pre-integrated soft-shadow texture** that transport taps **once**. So the cost moves from the per-pixel sample loop to per-sample bake passes (gated — the park pays nothing); the **leaves sit on their twigs** and the **twigs bridge branch to leaf** (the offset scales with real height, so the connecting wood lands between the branch shadow and the leaf shadow — no more invisible fine wood). Three details make it read right: the wood capsule is built **⊥ the shadow spine** (project both endpoints first, then lay the width across the streak) so a vertical trunk reads **round at every azimuth**, not a flat ribbon; the coherent sway **scales with height** (0 at the ground, full at the crown, for leaves *and* wood) so the tree **bends as one piece anchored at its foot** rather than sliding bodily; and the leaves **hug the twig** — distributed *along* the twig heading back from the tip with a tight perpendicular jitter, not an isotropic blob (the layer/park scatter stays the old blob, byte-identical). **Under wind the faithful canopy carries all three bands (§5.4) — and its medium band pivots on a *grown* joint, not a synthetic one.** It did not at first, for an honest reason worth keeping: the baked wood twigs rotated about the *limb*, while the leaf model pivoted about a *synthetic* stem joint (the §5.1 anti-vortex joint, built from `stem_length`) that is **not** the wood twig's real base; driving the leaves by a twig bend the wood couldn't share slid them off their now-*visible, opaque* twigs (the spec's "leaves sit on their twigs" must hold under motion, not just at rest), so the band was dropped here and registration bought with stillness. The cure was not a smaller amplitude but **one shared joint**: growth now records each terminal twig's **real base** — the point where it attaches to its parent — and *both* the leaves and that twig's *own wood* swing about **that** point, by the same per-twig angle, in the same order (twig about its base, then twig-and-leaves together about the limb, as on a real tree). Registration holds **by construction** rather than by luck, and the anti-vortex lesson survives untouched: a grown twig's base is, by definition, offset from its clump *toward the parent*, so it already **is** a stem joint — the layer path has to invent one (`stem_length`) only because it has no wood to borrow from. The band rides the existing `twig_flex` (0 = no twig motion) and rests at zero bend, so the still image is unchanged. Two more honesties: the **cast frame is bounded by the real plan extent of the leaves + wood** (not an assumed `±canopy_extent/2` box, which clipped a wide grove's outer crown), padded for the sway/drift; and the **faithful wood rim is a deliberately tight anti-alias** (not `edge_softness`, which models a *leaf* half-covering a texel) so thin twigs stay opaque — the wood's soft penumbra comes from the per-sample disk integration, not the rim.

**Fullness is leaf *size*, not count.** A subtle but load-bearing tuning fact (it is what makes a species read full vs. naked): a cluster's shadow is its **areal coverage** = (leaf count × leaf area) ⁄ cluster-disc area. `cluster_spread` is fixed while `leaf_size` varies, so a small-leaf species scatters as thin specks that never tile the cluster — the canopy reads **naked** (a dark skeleton with ghost-faint leaves) however many leaves you add, because the wood is opaque (`branch_tau`) and a sparse cluster casts ~80% transmittance. **Leaf size is the strong lever** (coverage ∝ area); raising the count barely moves it. So the `TREE_SPECIES` bundles carry per-species `leaf_size` / `foliage_density` tuned for *coverage*, not a raw count.

**The leader — monopodial growth (`leader_strength`), replacing the single-origin hub.** Originally every limb grew from one point (`[0,0,0]`), fanned evenly around a full circle — the literal definition of a dandelion, and the reason every tree read as the same spoke-burst (the tree-architecture research names this the core fault: a single hub is botanically correct *only* for a palm). A first cosmetic patch ("de-dandelion") slid the *drawn* limb bases off the hub to hide it, but the topology was still one fork and the branches floated, disconnected from the trunk — visible in the grove preview. That hack is **retired** for the real mechanism: **`leader_strength`** makes the trunk a **continuing axis** and attaches the limbs *along it* at staggered heights, so the branches actually meet the trunk. High leader → origins spread up the bole and limbs shorten toward the top (acrotony → the excurrent **cone** of a conifer); low-but-nonzero → a few near-base forks and a spreading **decurrent dome** (oak). This is the master cross-species axis (**apical control**) the model entirely lacked. It is gated: `leader_strength = 0` keeps the legacy single-hub growth (the palm/lollipop) exactly, so old looks are unchanged; a look opts in. Two couplings handled: child *and* limb azimuth switch to a **golden-angle phyllotaxis spiral** (no even spokes) when a leader is set, while `limbDir` (the wind-torque pivot, §5.1) stays on a **balanced fan** so a gust still leans rather than spins (decoupling the rendered divergence angle from the mechanical pivot); and a monopodial tree **carries its own height** (limbs are up the bole), so it scales z with the plan and stands on the ground rather than being lifted by the legacy `trunkH`. First realized in `park 1`. *(Step 1 of the broader tree-architecture work from the research brief — the excurrent/decurrent leader.)*

**Droop & upsweep — branches curve, they aren't straight rays (`droop`).** Step 2. A branch in the model was a single straight segment; real branches **curve continuously** along their length — a willow's twigs sag into a cascade, a conifer's sweep up. So `grow()` now splits a bending branch into a few **sub-segments** and tilts the heading a little at each (toward −z for sag, +z for upsweep), accumulating a **smooth arc** rather than a straight ray or a single hinge/kink. The bend is in the branch's **own vertical plane** (azimuth preserved — gravitropic, not a sideways twist), and children inherit the curved **end-tangent**, so the sag **compounds toward the tips**. `droop` is signed (−1 upsweep … 0 straight … +1 weep) and weighted by branch order (`level/levels`), so the main limbs stay relatively straight while the fine outer twigs curve most — the birch trail / willow fountain for `>0`, the conifer/elm-vase lift for `<0`. The sub-segment count scales with `|droop|` (straight branches stay one segment and pay nothing; `droop = 0` is byte-identical). It only moves twig *positions* within the same twig set, so it is a **canopy deformation** that morphs cleanly. *(Step 2.)*

**Taper — branches thin like wood, not wire (`taper_delta`).** Step 3, and the dominant "tree vs. wireframe" cue in the backlit silhouette. The drawn branch half-width was a flat ratio per order; real wood **conserves cross-section at each fork** — Leonardo's rule / the pipe model, `width_child = width_parent · children^(−1/Δ)` with Δ≈2 (each unit of foliage fed by a sapwood "pipe", so diameter ∝ √(load)). So the branch occluder's width now shrinks by `children^(−1/Δ)` per level: a stout trunk tapering to fine twigs. `taper_delta` is the exponent — **high Δ = slow-thinning stout limbs** (oak), **low Δ = fast-thinning lacy periphery** (birch). It touches only the **drawn branch shadow** (the trunk plan-projects to a point, leaves are separate), so it is **silhouette-only, zero dapple cost** — a pure backlit-read knob. *(Step 3.)*

**Crown aspect & phyllotaxis (`crown_aspect`, `phyllotaxis`).** Two more shape axes. **`crown_aspect`** scales the crown's **height** (>1 tall-narrow spire, <1 broad dome). In the **layer** cast it is invisible (the height-binding normalizes a uniform z-scale away → silhouette/preview only); in the **faithful** cast it changes the *real* shadow, so the tree's proportions read in what it throws. Decoupling height from the **plan-fill** scale was the prerequisite: the per-tree normalize stretches the plan so the widest twig fills the crown radius (`s = crown/mr`), and the old code scaled height by that *same* `s` — which **explodes** for a narrow-plan species (steep branches → tiny `mr` → `s` of 6+), making `columnar` compute to ~47 m once the faithful cast used real heights. So the height scale **floors the plan radius** (`FAITH_MAX_RATIO`): a broad tree's height is untouched (its `mr` is above the floor), a narrow one is **capped** at `crown · RATIO · aspect` so it can't run away. **`phyllotaxis`** sets how children fan around a parent: **spiral** (golden angle, the even non-repeating fill of most trees — the default), **whorled** (an even ring per node → conifer tiers), and **opposite** (children forced to a pair 180° apart, the node-plane flipped 90° each level → the maple/ash decussate candelabra of stacked Y-forks). Both feed `grow()` and the limb loop.

**`TREE_SPECIES` — the selector (the inverse of `WIND_PATTERNS`).** The payoff. A table of **named shape bundles** (`oak`, `spruce`, `birch`, `willow`, `maple`, `columnar`, `palm`), each a partial set of the growth/shape knobs (`leader_strength`, `droop`, `taper_delta`, `crown_aspect`, `phyllotaxis` + the structural `branch_*`/`limb_count`/`foliage_density`/`leaf_size`). Where `WIND_PATTERNS` shapes a *force*, `TREE_SPECIES` shapes *growth*. Crucially it is **shape ⟂ scene**: the editor merges the chosen bundle **over the current look's params** (lighting, camera, wind untouched), stamps `tree_species` as an **inert label** (the engine never reads it — all behavior comes from the expanded knobs), and rebuilds. So one click turns the standing tree into an oak or a spruce on whatever scene you're in, and every knob stays hand-tweakable afterward (it then reads "custom"). It lives in `presets.js` as data (`export const TREE_SPECIES`), like the looks; the species' structural knobs are `TOPO`, so a species swap rebuilds (and, in a transition, dissolves through the cloud). *(The brief's `palm` proves the point: it **is** komorebi's original single-hub model — `leader_strength 0` — so the old piña-colada wasn't wrong, just stuck rendering every tree as a palm.)* *(Still to come from the research: per-level shape arrays (per-order angle/length/count), multi-stem/clumping, and the flat-topped savanna form.)*

**Texture constraints:** it must resolve the **smallest gaps** (they throw the sharpest sun-images); it must **extend past the visible ground by the largest layer shift** (so dapples don't run out of canopy at the frame edge); and it should **tile** if wind is to scroll it indefinitely.

### 4.6 Transport

One equation:

> **A ground point's color = Σ over source samples of [ sample weight × Π over layers of transmittance(read at a shifted position) ].**

- The **shift for layer L = its height × the sample's angular offset.** High layers shift more, so high foliage blurs more — depth-dependent bokeh, automatically.
- **Multiplying transmittance across layers** is the physically correct "light must clear every layer."
- **The ellipse lives here.** Convert each angular sample-offset into a *ground-plane* offset through a small **2×2 matrix built from the sun's elevation and azimuth**. That matrix stretches the circular source-disk into the ground ellipse and shears the pattern toward the sun. The source stays a clean circle; transport owns the projection; the two never tangle.
- **Leaf-edge diffraction splits the channels (optional, §3.6).** With it on, each colour channel reads the layer at its own wavelength-scaled shift — red wider, blue narrower — so the dapple edges fringe warm-out / cool-in. With it off the three reads collapse to one shared tap, so every existing look is byte-identical and pays nothing.
- **Accumulate in linear HDR; tone-map at the very end.** The contrast/exposure knob lives downstream of the physics, so the look can be tuned without touching the light.

### 4.7 Look

Elliptical projection (from transport), the green wash (from the canopy), the **physical sun & sky colour** (`uSunColor`/`uAmbient` from the §3.5 atmosphere, driven by `sun_elevation` + `sky_turbidity`), the **ground albedo** the floor reflects with (`ground_rgb` — the irradiance that lands is reflected off a surface reflectance rather than an implicit white one; `(1,1,1)`, the default, is the plain white floor, and a few looks set a warm Mount-Royal dirt so their deep shade reads as dark earth while only the hottest dapples burn back toward white), a **tilted perspective camera** (`view_pitch_deg` / `view_fov_deg` — a pinhole that casts each pixel onto the floor; at pitch 0 it reduces exactly to the original orthographic top-down map, and tilting drops the near floor to the bottom of frame, recedes the far floor, and dissolves it into atmospheric haze; `view_extent_m` stays the zoom = the on-axis ground span. A **camera orbit** (`view_yaw_deg`) rotates the sampled floor about frame centre — a true orbit for a flat floor: the tilt/foreshortening axis stays put while the world spins under it, applied as a 2×2 rotation of the ground point *before* the sun-shift, so the sun-shift `g` stays in world space and the shadows remain physically cast as the gaze turns; 0 = identity, byte-identical. A **camera pan** (`view_center_x/y`) then slides the world point the screen centre looks at (0,0 = frame centre), added to the ground point after the yaw — so you can walk the view across the floor, e.g. push the tree out of frame and watch only what it casts. Pitch + yaw + pan + zoom together let you walk around the structure to make sense of it. Camera height is omitted — for a flat floor it only rescales the view, which the zoom hold cancels, so it has no independent effect. A **far-field smear** (`far_smear`) then widens the soft-shadow throw by each pixel's ground footprint, which balloons toward the horizon — det(∂world/∂vUv) = `view_extent²·cos⁴pitch·aspect / D³` with `D = −ray.z`, so the footprint's linear size goes as `1/D^1.5`; realised by adding an `extraThrow` to every layer's height *inside* the existing `uProj` shift, so far dapples melt into soft **down-sun** streaks and the far-field aliasing/shimmer is averaged out while the near floor stays crisp. Referenced to the nearest visible row (`D_ref = cos pitch + tan(fov/2)·sin pitch`) it is *exactly* 0 at pitch 0 — a uniform footprint has no near/far gradient — so top-down presets stay byte-identical and the smear is purely a tilted-gaze phenomenon that strengthens with tilt), exposure and contrast tied to source width, and a final tone-map — all **downstream** of the physics, accumulated in linear HDR.

### 4.8 Standing scene & the trunk (the tree as an object, not an overhead slab)

The park model treats the canopy as a **thin horizontal slab hovering overhead**, grown to fill the frame: you are lying *under* an endless canopy and the dapples are everywhere. That is faithful to §1 — but it hides a simplification that betrays any look where a tree is meant to be *seen standing in a scene* (the bedroom-curtain north star, and ordinary "a tree on the grass casting its shadow across the floor toward me"):

**The missing physics: there is no bulk lateral shadow offset.** §4.6's `uProj` maps the *sample offsets* of the source disk to ground displacements — it stretches and shears the dapples into ellipses — but the **centre** of the source (sample 0) maps to **zero shift**. So every shadow lands **directly under** its occluder, only stretched; the sun's *angle* never throws the shadow off to the side. For an infinite, uniform overhead canopy this is invisible — a bulk translation of an endless field shows nothing — which is exactly why the park model never needed it and still doesn't. But it has two consequences the moment the tree is a discrete object:

- **The trunk casts no streak.** A real trunk's shadow is the long line `height × cot(elevation)` *away* from the sun. With no bulk offset, a vertical trunk's "shadow" collapses to a small fuzzy blob at its own foot (just the ellipse spread) — a circle, not a streak.
- **The shadow sits on top of the tree.** Without the lateral throw, the dappled crown shadow lands right under the crown, never "cast over there."

**`standing_scene`** is an opt-in mode (off by default → the park model, byte-identical) that adds the missing offset: a bulk ground shift **`cot(elevation) · (anti-sun direction)` per unit height**, folded into the per-sample shift `g` for every occluder — layers, branches, and the trunk alike. Now a low sun throws the whole tree's shadow to the side: trunk streak → branch shadows → dappled crown blob, marching `height × cot(elevation)` down-sun. (It is gated to this mode precisely because for the park's frame-filling, edge-clamped canopy a bulk shift would only drag the pattern off its texture — invisible benefit, real cost — so the park looks keep it off and stay byte-identical.)

**The trunk is part of the woody occluder (§4.5).** A vertical trunk has **no horizontal footprint**, so the layered slab cannot cast it — which is *why* the woody shadow (trunk + main limbs) is the continuous-height analytic occluder of §4.5 rather than a stamp. Standing-scene just supplies the **bulk offset** in the central sun direction it casts along (`trunk_radius_m` sets the base width; the radius tapers by branch order). So the trunk's streak and the limbs' shadows are one connected piece that rakes across the floor with a low sun, sways with the leaves, and composes with the camera orbit/pan for free. *(In `faithful_canopy` mode (§4.5) the trunk + branches + twigs are instead baked as real geometry at their grown heights — the capsule laid **across the shadow streak** so the trunk reads round at every azimuth, its base **planted** while the crown sways. The analytic occluder here is the layer-mode wood.)*

**The whole shadow is one connected thing — don't centre on a piece of it.** A dead end worth recording: a first attempt *auto-centred the camera on the crown shadow* (`view centre += −crown_height·bulk`). It looked wrong, and the reason is physical. The trunk's foot is at height 0, so it never shifts; the crown is high, so it shifts most; pinning the crown at screen-centre with the camera therefore made the **trunk slide out from under its own crown** as the sun moved — the tree's shadow visibly came apart. The trunk and crown are one rigid object; their shadow must sweep **together**. The cure is to apply the bulk offset to **every occluder via the shared `g`** (trunk, branches, leaves) and **leave the camera alone** — then lowering the sun stretches the entire connected shadow (foot → trunk → branches → crown) as one, which is exactly right.

**Framing the off-frame tree is the camera's job, not a trick.** With the tree baked at the world origin, a **fixed camera pan** (`view_center_x/y`) simply looks at the patch of floor *in front of* the tree, so the tree's foot sits off the frame and only its cast shadow lies in view — and orbit/pan pivot on that floor patch, not the trunk. As the sun moves the shadow sweeps across the fixed view (raise it past overhead and the shadow retreats under the off-frame tree, leaving lit floor — correct); the user pans to re-frame. This is honest: physics moves the shadow, the camera is just where you stand. *(The editor offers a **"frame shadow"** convenience that computes a one-time `view_center`/`view_extent` from the bulk throw × a representative crown height, dropping you near the shadow-only composition to nudge from — a one-shot camera move, explicitly **not** the per-frame auto-centre-on-crown the dead-end above warns against.)*

**The lit floor and the discrete tree.** To read a shadow on a lit floor rather than a wall of dapples, the canopy must not fill the view: a **wide `view_extent`** shows lots of floor, and (standing-scene only) the crown is sized to a **natural fraction of the bake extent** rather than inflated to fill the frame — so a single tree is a finite object whose crown fits its texture, with the empty, clamp-to-edge surround reading as fully-lit floor. No new geometry — the existing grove, made a discrete tree, its connected shadow swept aside by the bulk offset, the camera parked on the floor it falls on.

First realized as the `park 1` look (the former `afternoon 8`): a wide, low-sun, finite grove whose trunk-and-crown shadow rakes across a lit floor. The mode is meant to **eventually apply to every preset** as an option, not just the park looks. *(Next refinements: rooting the trees fully outside the frame for a pure "shadow only" composition, and — from the §-tree-architecture research — the excurrent/decurrent shape work so the standing tree reads as a species, not a piña-colada.)*

### 4.9 The receiver — the surface the light lands on (the curtain)

Everything through §4.8 ends at one line. Transport builds **`acc`** — the dimensionless fraction of the sun's disk that cleared every occluder at a ground point, i.e. the **landed irradiance** — and §4.7's Look turns it into colour with `(acc·uSunColor + uAmbient)·uGround`. That single multiply *is* a receiver: an opaque diffuse floor, reflecting the landed light off an albedo. It was fused inline and never named.

The **bedroom curtain** (§1's second north star — sunset through a backyard tree, onto a moss-velvet drape, seen from the bed) forces the receiver to become a **subsystem of its own**. A translucent woven screen does not *reflect* the landed light; it *transmits* it, lit from behind. So "Look/ground" is promoted to a swappable **receiver**: *opaque diffuse floor* and *translucent scattering fabric* are two implementations of one interface — **take the arriving irradiance and the surface geometry, return the radiance toward the eye**. The seam is exactly that fused line; the gate is `receiver` (0 = floor, byte-identical; 1 = curtain).

**The coupling, stated once: geometry up, material down.** The receiver's *geometry* (its plane and normal, and later the fold displacement) feeds **up** into transport — it sets where the shadow projects and which surface the ray meets. The *material* (transmit, scatter, dye, weave, pile) flows **down** from the landed `acc`. This is the §4.8 lesson reread for the receiver: occluder and receiver must share one geometry or the shadow comes apart.

**The transmission (the moss velvet).** The fabric answers the landed irradiance by **transmitting** it through dyed pile. The discipline that keeps a dark velvet from reading as a bright tinted gel is a clean split: **total throughput `Tt ∈ [0,1]` carries brightness; the dye carries only hue.** The dye `tint` is normalized to unit peak (`tint̂ = tint / max-channel`) — exactly the `atmosphere()` "normalize to HUE; exposure carries brightness" idiom (§3.5), the textile twin of the leaf-transmittance chlorophyll spectrum (§4.5) — so

> curtain colour = (acc·uSunColor + uAmbient) · (Tt · tint̂)

is the **structural twin of the floor**, with a transmittance `Tt·tint̂` standing where the albedo `uGround` stood. It is energy-bounded (`Tt·tint̂ ≤ 1`, so the transmit can never out-glow the landed light) and it **collapses byte-identically** to the floor when `Tt·tint̂ = uGround`. The ambient skylight is **single-counted** — it is part of the landed irradiance and is gated through `Tt·tint̂` once, like the beam (the sky that leaks through a backlit curtain is itself attenuated and dyed by the cloth). The look the curtain wants falls straight out of composition with the warm low sun: near-white gap light reads warm-gold, the green dye × low `Tt` reads as a dim olive body glow, and the deep shade where only sky leaks reads cool green-black — komorebi's "suns stay white-gold, shade goes dim green," moved from floor to fabric. *(Do not add the ambient a second time (`… + uAmbient·Tt`): that double-counts the skylight and breaks both the energy bound and the byte-identical collapse. The single-count form above is the law.)*

**The standing plane (the shadow stands up the cloth).** The curtain is a *vertical* receiver, and the soft-shadow read generalizes to it for almost free. Transport's floor read shifts each occluder layer by `height · g` (`g` the per-unit-height ellipse+bulk shift of §4.6/§4.8) and reads it under the floor point. For *any* planar receiver the only change is that the height is measured **relative to the receiver point**: read layer at height `h` shifted by `(h − recvZ) · g`, where `recvZ` is the receiver point's own height. On the floor `recvZ = 0` and it is the old read, byte-identical; on the curtain `recvZ` is the height **up the cloth**, so an occluder a height `(h − recvZ)` above a given cloth point throws its shadow exactly there — the tree's dapples and its trunk/branch streaks **stand up the vertical plane**, projected nearly upright, instead of lying flat on the floor. The camera, in curtain mode, is a head-on view of that plane (screen → the cloth's own `u` across / `v` up); the **bulk sun-throw of §4.8 is what rakes the projection**, so a low sun is what makes the shadow climb the cloth, and the plane's distance behind the tree (`cloth_distance_m`) plus the sun angle place where it lands. *(This is the §4.8 "occluder and receiver share one geometry" lesson **honoured**, not dodged — the receiver's height enters the very shift that casts the shadow. Only **folds** — a receiver that corrugates per-pixel — remain the hard v2 part; a flat vertical plane is not.)* The faithful bake of §4.5 re-aims at the cloth the same way — both leaves and wood project into `uCurtainBake`/`uClothY`'s cloth coords (`u = px − r·gx, v = h − r` with `r=(py−cy)/gy`), and the CPU side sweeps the plan-AABB corners × heights through each sample's cloth map to bound the cast frame — this is the faithful curtain path. The **layer** occluder path also generalizes via the same height-minus-recvZ read and serves as the cheaper fallback tier, with one approximation: in layer/analytic mode the woody occluder's capsule distance on the curtain is measured partly in plan-depth rather than up-the-cloth, so its penumbra there is approximate.

**Both regimes in one frame (§3.2 on the curtain).** On the curtain the §3.2 crossover reappears with **receiver-distance `d` in place of canopy-height `h`**: a feature is in the *shape* regime when its aperture `a ≫ d·θ` and the *pinhole/sun-image* regime when `a ≪ d·θ`. The window's 30–40 cm mullion panes, a few centimetres off the cloth, are deep shape regime — **you see the grid, sharp**; the tree's leaf-gaps, metres away, are pinhole — **soft sun-images**. Same sun, same frame, opposite regimes, set purely by `d`. This is the curtain's defining image, and it needs no new transport: the regimes fall out of each occluder's height feeding the per-sample shift.

**The pleat field (one function: vertical, aperiodic, C∞).** A flat cloth reads as a screen, not a curtain — so the receiver hangs in **pleats**, and every consumer of them reads **one** displacement function of the cloth coordinate, which returns the out-of-plane shape and its `u`-derivative together. Gravity writes the first rule: cloth *hangs*, a pleat runs down the drape parallel to the pull, and nothing about a heavier hem makes it lean — so the phase of every harmonic is a function of `u` **alone**, and the ridges are exactly vertical at every height.

> `Δy(u,v) = fold_warp · Σᵢ wᵢ(v)·aᵢ·sin(kᵢ·u·2π + φᵢ) / Σᵢ wᵢ(v)·aᵢ`,  with  `kᵢ = fold_scale · (1, 1/φ, 1/(1+√2))`

Three harmonics at **pairwise irrational** ratios — the golden and silver means against the fundamental. Their sum is *almost-periodic*: it never repeats, at any distance, so the drape has no wallpaper tile and no two pleats are alike. That is aperiodicity for **free** — no hash, no noise texture, no RNG, nothing frame-unstable — and, unlike any of those, C∞ smooth, which the warp's derivative and the incidence normal below both require of it. The amplitudes go as `aᵢ ∝ 1/kᵢ`, so every harmonic contributes the *same* peak slope while the displacement stays broad-dominated: the eye reads big soft waves carrying fine gathers, and the shading — which reads slope, not height — gets an even contribution from each octave.

**Coarsening is a spectral reweighting, not a chirp.** `fold_coarsen` fades the octaves against each other down the drape — `w = (f, 1, 2−f)` with `f = 1/(1 + fold_coarsen·v)` — so the finest harmonic **dies out** toward the hem while the broadest grows to take its place: fine gathers at the rod melting into broad waves at the floor, ridges vertical throughout, which is what a heavy drape actually does. `fold_coarsen = 0` gives `w = (1,1,1)`, the same mixture top to bottom. The `Σ wᵢaᵢ` divisor holds the peak displacement at `fold_warp` **metres at every height**, so the knob keeps meaning metres while the mixture underneath it changes (measured: 99.9 % of `fold_warp` reached at every height sampled).

*Do not coarsen by chirping the frequency.* A single sinusoid at `k(v) = fold_scale/√(1 + fold_coarsen·v)` states a truth about a heavy drape's *wavelength* in exactly the wrong place. A ridge is a curve of **constant phase**, and constant phase under a chirp means `u·k(v) = const`, i.e. **`u ∝ √(1 + c·v)`** — every ridge tilts *outward* as it descends, by an amount proportional to its own `u`. The cloth grows a symmetric diagonal fan spreading from the frame centre, and on screen it reads as **light on the bottom of a swimming pool** — the caustic fan, not fabric. Hanging cloth cannot do that; a drape coarsens by fine pleats *dying out*, never by leaning. The rule generalizes past this shader: *a spatially varying frequency is a statement about phase, and phase is where the geometry lives* — where a feature's orientation is physically fixed, vary **which frequencies are present** and never the frequency itself. The octave crossfade is that rule obeyed, and the incommensurate harmonics it needs supply the aperiodicity as a side effect.

**The velvet (the shading half).** `fold_depth` is authored pile thickness painted onto a flat plane, and the pleats' actual out-of-plane displacement is a separate knob (`fold_warp`, the fold warp below); the two are deliberately not one control, because one shades and the other moves the cloth. Its 0..1 driver is the field's own **normalized slope**, `facing = |dΔŷ/du| / max|dΔŷ/du|` — taken from the *unit* shape, before `fold_warp` scales it to metres, so the banding is authored pile and survives on a cloth with no displacement at all. The shading turns a sheet into thick velvet two ways — the edge-on fold flanks present more backlit pile so they transmit less (dark vertical bands, `fold_depth`), and the face-on ridges catch a soft pale **sheen** (a desaturated warm tint of the dye, two-tone over the dim body — `velvet_sheen`), the giveaway of a standing-fibre cut pile rather than a flat dyed weave. *(No flat warp×weft tooth: a thin woven grid reads as canvas, not terciopelo.)* `fold_depth = 0` is the flat cloth; `velvet_sheen = 0` is matte.

**The forward-scatter wrap (the velvet's body glow — the per-pixel tier).** Backlit cut pile transmits by **two** routes, and the drape above modelled only one. The **ballistic** component threads the pile directly: it is highly directional, so an edge-on fold flank — several times more fibre along the ray — extinguishes it, and that is precisely the `fold_depth` banding. The rest **forward-scatters**: it diffuses among the fibres and leaves the back face in a broad forward lobe, near-isotropic, so which way the flank turns barely attenuates it. `fabric_scatter = s ∈ [0,1]` is the **share of the transmitted light that takes the second route**, and it enters as a *split of the same energy, never an add*:

> body · **mix(band, bandSoft, s)** · nap + sheen,  with  band = e^(−`fold_depth`·facing),  bandSoft = e^(−`fold_depth`·facing·0.25)

The scattered share walks the same Beer path at a **quarter** weight — it still dims edge-on (there is still more pile to cross) but it never goes black, so the body glow **wraps** into the dark flanks and the cloth reads as lit from *within* rather than as a stencil. The 0.25 is an authored *"mostly, not wholly, orientation-blind"* choice, not a measurement: 0 would flatten the scattered glow perfectly, 1 would collapse it back onto the ballistic band and the wrap would do nothing. **The energy bound holds by construction** — both bands are ≤ 1, so their mix is, so `Tt·tint̂·mix ≤ 1` still forbids the cloth out-glowing the light that landed on it; this is why the term is a mix and not the tempting `+ s·glow`. The dye is not doubled either: the scattered light crossed the *same* dyed pile, so the hue stays `tint̂` and there is no separate scatter tint. `s = 0` is **byte-identical** to the shading above (the mix collapses to `band`), and the cost is one `exp` and one `mix` per pixel inside a branch the floor never enters — no profiler axis.

**What the wrap cannot do — the line between the two tiers.** Stated plainly, because it is exactly where the cheap rung ends and the expensive one begins: with no neighbour reads, the wrap redistributes light **angularly at a single point**. It therefore softens the **fold** shading — a per-pixel function of that pleat's own orientation — and that is the whole reason it works for free. It **cannot bleed light across a cast shadow's edge**: a dapple's glow spilling a centimetre into the shade beside it is a *lateral* transport of energy between neighbouring cloth points, and nothing that reads only its own pixel can produce it. That second effect is a true 2-D diffusion, it must read neighbours in linear HDR, and it is the tier realized below, at that cost. Per §9's quality-rung contract: the diffusion tier degrades to **this wrap**, and the wrap degrades to `s = 0`, off.

**The window mullion grid (the near-contact occluder).** The grid is the *authored* occluder — the inverse of the grown canopy — and it is the half of *"both regimes in one frame"* above that was missing: a rigid lattice of vertical and horizontal bars, pitch `mullion_pitch_m` (the pane), width `mullion_bar_m`, standing `mullion_depth_m` in **front** of the cloth on the sun side. It is evaluated **analytically per pixel in cloth `(u,v)`** — the near-contact sibling of §4.5's woody occluder, a few ALU, no texture, no geometry, no regrow.

*Do not stamp the grid into a depth layer.* On the faithful path transport is a **single pre-integrated texture tap** (`tapFaith`) and **the layer loop never runs**, so a layer-stamped grid is invisible in exactly the looks that need it (§4.5 bars the layer-stamped *branch* shadow for the sibling reason: discrete-height quantization shatters continuous lines at a low sun). *"Composites through the existing tap" is only free while there is a loop to composite into.* An **analytic occluder in receiver coordinates** has no such dependency — it runs identically on the faithful tier and the layer fallback tier, because it is applied at the receiver, after transport, where both tiers have already collapsed to one `acc`.

**Throw — the grid rakes with the sun.** The bar plane sits at world-Y `cy + d`, so along the central ray it drops `r = d/gy` to reach the cloth (`g` = the bulk per-unit-height shift of §4.8, `gy` its cloth-facing component). That is the *same* projection the faithful bake casts with — `u = px − r·gx, v = h − r` — inverted to ask which bar point shadows a given cloth point: **`(u,v) + r·(gx, 1)`**, one vector up-sun. So a bar's shadow slides in the same direction the trunk's does as the sun moves, just ~1/60th as far at these depths, which is precisely what sells the bars as *in front of* the cloth rather than printed on it. A single central ray is used rather than a per-sample integration, and that is safe here for the reason the whole feature exists: at centimetre `d` the spread across the source disk is **sub-millimetre on the cloth**, and the penumbra term below already stands in for it.

**Penumbra — depth × the source, and nothing else.** The soft edge is `mullion_depth_m × (core angular radius · (1 + 3·cloud_thickness))` — the woody occluder's `uOccPenumbra` idiom **minus its `1/sin(elevation)` factor**, deliberately: that factor is ground obliquity, and the cloth is viewed *head-on*, so a bar's shadow is never smeared along a receding plane. A clear sun therefore draws the grid crisp and drifting cloud honestly softens it — §3.4's breathing reaches the window through the same widened source that softens everything else, at no extra cost.

**Beam versus hemisphere — what a near occluder actually takes.** The woody occluder multiplies **`acc` alone**: wood metres away blocks only the sun's small disk, and the skylight still reaches around it. A bar *centimetres* off the cloth subtends nearly the **entire hemisphere** seen from the point behind it, so it takes the beam *and* the sky. The mullion transmittance `exp(−τ·coverage)` therefore multiplies the **whole landed irradiance `E = acc·uSunColor + uAmbient`**, inside the receiver branch, before the fabric answers it. This is not a shortcut for the far case nor an over-reach for the near one — it is the honest difference between a far occluder and a near-contact one, and it is why the shaded panes read as genuinely dark instead of merely un-sunlit.

Honest approximations, stated: the bars are infinitely thin in depth (no jamb thickness, no reveal shadow); and `mullion_tau = 0` is the gate, byte-identical, with the whole evaluation living inside the `receiver ≠ 0` branch so the floor path costs nothing. *(The window aperture below bounds the grid — a wall has no glazing.)*

**The window aperture (the light gets an edge).** Without the aperture the light behind the cloth is **infinite**: a lit field with no boundary, tiled edge to edge by a uniform fishnet of bars, and monotone by construction — nothing in the frame is dark because nothing in the frame is *wall*. §1's scene is the opposite shape. There is a **finite bright rectangle** of sky and a dark room around it, and that single macro contrast is most of the composition; every subtlety the receiver models — the pleat shading, the velvet's body glow, the dapples — is legible only *against* it. So the aperture: width `window_w_m` × height `window_h_m`, centred at (`window_cx_m`, `window_cy_m`) in the cloth's own coordinates, a soft-edged rectangle multiplying the landed irradiance.

It lives at the **mullion's bar plane** and is read at the **same warped cast coordinate** the bars are, because it is not a separate object: the aperture edge is the window's outermost frame member. So it takes the identical central-ray throw `(u,v) + (d/gy)·(gx, 1)`, and its soft edge is the identical `mullion_depth_m × source` penumbra — one physics, one uniform, frame and glazing bars softening together as cloud thickens (§3.4). The visible consequence is the point: **the frame's edge S-bends across the pleats exactly as the bars do**, because it is cast by the same geometry onto the same corrugated cloth. An aperture drawn straight while the bars inside it wave would announce itself instantly as a mask laid over the picture rather than a window seen through it.

> `mask = window_wall + (1 − window_wall)·insideRect(castUV + (d/gy)·(gx,1))`,  and `E ← E · mask`

It multiplies the **whole landed irradiance** `E`, beam and sky alike, at exactly the point `mullionT` does and for exactly the same reason — a wall centimetres off the cloth subtends nearly the entire hemisphere behind that point, so it shuts out the skylight as thoroughly as the sun (the near-contact argument stated for the bars above, applied to a much larger occluder). And the **grid is confined to the aperture**: the bars belong to the window, a wall has none, so the coverage the grid contributes is scaled by the same inside-rect factor. Without that the fishnet would keep crawling across a surface that has no glazing in it.

`window_wall` is the one deliberately non-physical term, and it earns its place: the wall really is opaque, so strictly nothing should land outside the aperture — but the room the viewer is *in* has its own dim light on the near side of the cloth, which this stands in for at a few percent. At zero the surround collapses to a black void and the drape stops existing there; at 0.03 it reads as dark cloth whose folds you can still just make out. The honest description is *a stand-in for the front-side room light this model does not otherwise have*, not an approximation of transmission through masonry. `window_w_m · window_h_m = 0` is the gate — an infinite lit field, byte-identical to every look before this one — and the bar grid's confinement factor is exactly `1` there, so the infinite grid is untouched too.

**The fold warp (the cast bends over the pleats).** The drape above shades folds onto a flat plane; `fold_warp` makes them **geometry**. A pleated cloth point does not sit on the plane `Y = cy` that every occluder read is built against — it stands `Δy` out of it — so it intercepts a *different ray*, and what lands on it is the flat cast read at a **shifted coordinate**. That shift is not a new derivation. From the same projection the bake casts with (`u = px − r·gx`, `v = h − r`, `r = (py−cy)/gy`), displacing the receiver by `Δy` changes `r` by `Δy/gy` — which is **exactly the mullion throw** above, `cloth + (depth/gy)·(gx, 1)`, one central ray up-sun, with the pleat displacement standing in for the bar depth. The sign convention is what makes that identity literal: **`Δy > 0` bulges the pleat toward the room**, away from the sun side, so the flat-cast plane sits in *front* of the displaced point precisely as the bars do.

> `castUV = clothUV + (Δy/gy)·(gx, 1)`,  with `Δy` **the same** displacement the pleat field above hands the drape

**The shared phase is a requirement, not tidiness.** The drape's `facing` is the *normalized magnitude of that field's own derivative*, so it peaks exactly where `Δy` is steepest and the S-bend lands on the fold **flanks** — the tilted parts, the only places a displacement *should* bend anything — instead of on the ridges. Two copies of the corrugation would drift apart the moment either was edited and the bend would slide off the folds it is bending over. With one function returning `(Δŷ, dΔŷ/du)` and one evaluation per pixel feeding both consumers, the invariant is **structural**: there is no second copy to drift.

**What warps, and what deliberately does not.** `castUV` replaces the cloth coordinate everywhere the **incoming light field** is sampled: the faithful texture tap; the layer tier's cloth read, carried as `world.x` + `recvZ` — the layer maps are plan-space, so sliding the receiver point along its own ray is *exact* here rather than a stand-in, and the analytic woody occluder follows for free because it reads the same two; and the mullion grid's cloth argument, where the composition is right by construction — a displaced pleat sees the bars at depth `d + Δy`, and `mullionT` adds its own `d/gy` on top of the warped coordinate. The window's straight glazing bars **S-bending across the pleats** is this feature's defining image. What stays unwarped is the **shading** coordinate: the pleats are attached to the cloth, so `velvetCloth` still reads `clothUV`. Only the arriving pattern warps — warping both would slide the folds off themselves.

Honest approximations, stated: the warp **re-samples a cast baked against a flat plane**, so it is a *first-order* bend — it does not re-run transport against the corrugated surface. The occluder-to-cloth distance really varies by ±`fold_warp` across a pleat and the penumbra should breathe with it; here every flank keeps the softness the flat plane gave it, and a steep enough flank would self-shadow, which nothing models. Like the mullion it uses the **central ray only**, so the bend does not vary across the source disk. The warped read can also land just outside the baked cast frame (by `|fold_warp/gy|` metres — sub-centimetre at any sane amplitude), where the texture clamp answers. `fold_warp = 0` is the flat cloth, byte-identical, and the cost is one `sin` inside a branch the floor never enters.

**The fold incidence basis (the flanks take the real sun angle).** A tilted flank is lit by how squarely it meets the sun, and that **incidence cosine** is the one piece of curtain geometry the engine's existing sun vectors cannot supply. `uProj` and `uBulkShift` are *throw rates per unit height*: both carry `1/sin(elevation)`, both diverge as the sun nears the horizon, and both are floored (4° and 6°) to stay finite. Incidence on a **vertical** cloth does the exact opposite — it is `cos(el)·|sin(az)|`, which **peaks** as the sun lowers head-on toward the window, precisely where those two are least trustworthy. Reusing them here would make the folds dimmest at the sunset the whole look is built for. So the CPU sends a **unit sun direction** instead, `(cos el·cos az, cos el·sin az, sin el)`, derived per frame from the sun angles already in hand and deliberately **unclamped in elevation** — the one sun vector in the shader that is a direction rather than a rate.

The flank normal comes from the **same** corrugation the warp displaces with — the pleat field returns `dΔy/du` alongside `Δy`, analytically, one harmonic sum differentiated term by term (`Σ wᵢaᵢ·kᵢ·2π·cos(kᵢu·2π + φᵢ) / Σ wᵢaᵢ`, scaled by `fold_warp`) — and with `Δy` measured toward the room the surface `(u, cy − Δy, v)` has unit normals `±(dΔy/du, 1, 0)/‖·‖`, the sun-facing one picked by the sign of the sun's own cloth-facing component. Then

> `cosI = max(n̂_sun · sunDir, 0)`,  and the shading factor is `cosI / cosI_flat`, normalized so a **flat** cloth is exactly `1.0`

which is why this needs **no new knob**: the gate is the warp geometry itself. `fold_warp = 0` means no tilt, no variation, byte-identical. Note what the normalization exposes — the elevation **cancels** in the ratio (it is common to both cosines), so what modulates the flanks is pure **azimuth**: a sun squarely head-on to the cloth (`az = 90°`) tilts both flanks away equally and only foreshortens them, and it takes an off-axis sun to produce true lit/dark banding, one flank turned into the beam and the other away. The ratio therefore **exceeds 1** on a flank facing the sun — honest, since that flank really does collect more than the flat plane would — and it diverges as the sun rakes parallel to the cloth, the same degenerate pose the cloth-space throw already guards, where the cast itself is meaningless. That is the one place the scatter split's `≤ 1` bound is **re-based rather than broken**: the split still divides one fixed transmitted energy, and the incidence corrects the *irradiance being divided* — `E` was only ever the flat plane's.

It multiplies the **ballistic** share only. The scattered share leaves the pile in a broad near-isotropic lobe after bouncing among the fibres, so which way the flank turns barely modulates it, while the ballistic component is directional by definition — that asymmetry is the same physics the forward-scatter split above rests on, applied at the entrance instead of the exit. And it lands **on top of** `fold_depth`, not in place of it: the authored band is pile *thickness*, this is *orientation*, and they are still two knobs because they serve two authorial roles. Deriving the band from the measured path length through pile at incidence — one physical model where the two collapse into each other — is the honest unification, and it is deliberately **not** done here; recorded as future polish rather than smuggled in. Recorded too: the normal has no vertical component, so the pleats are treated as strictly vertical corrugations. The octave crossfade does give `Δy` a slow `v`-dependence through the weights `w(v)`, hence a real `∂Δy/∂v` — small (the weights change over metres, the pleats over centimetres) and deliberately dropped, so the coarsening tilts nothing of its own.

**The tent — the receiver's second instance.** The interface generalizes as §4.9 promised it would, and the proof is a *second material* rather than a second mechanism. §1's other reference — komorebi cast on the nylon of a tent pitched under trees at midday — runs the **identical** receiver: the same transmission law, the same `Tt·tint̂` split, the same pleat field, the same warp and incidence. What changes is only the material point, and it is nearly the opposite corner of the space: the velvet is **low `Tt`, saturated dye, pile sheen, mostly ballistic**; thin nylon is **high `Tt`, near-neutral, matte, heavily forward-scattering**. That the same six knobs reach both without a branch is the interface earning its keep — and the tent's near-white cloth makes a point the velvet hides: the scene's **green comes from the leaves' transmittance**, not the receiver, because a fabric that barely dyes the light passes the canopy's own spectrum straight through. Its fold field is *wrinkles* rather than pleats (broad, shallow, barely coarsening), and its window and mullion gates stay at **zero** — the tent stands **under** the canopy, so the whole cloth is lit and the field is continuous dapples, with no aperture edge anywhere in it.

The tent is also where the **`(h − recvZ)·θ` depth blur is most legible**, and it gets there without the window. `curtain 1` staged both §3.2 regimes with an *authored* near-contact occluder centimetres off the cloth; the tent stages them with the **tree's own height spread** — branches nearly grazing the fabric cast hard-edged shapes in the same frame the canopy metres above casts soft plates. Same sun, same transport, the crossover set purely by each occluder's distance, exactly as §3.2 says — which is the cleanest demonstration the model has that the softness is *geometry* and not a blur radius. Recorded with it: the reference's **spotlight-versus-haze** contrast — some patches reading as hard bright spots, others as diffuse glow — is only partly reachable at preset level (a hot sun under thin cloud, plus exposure and contrast). The rest of it is a dapple's glow bleeding *across* its own shadow edge, which is the **lateral-diffusion tier** below; that photograph is the motivating image for it.

**The lateral diffusion (the glow crosses the shadow edge — the curtain's one expensive step).** The wrap above redistributes light *angularly* at a single point; this spreads it *spatially* between points, and it is the same forward scattering read one scale coarser: a photon entering the weave inside a hot dapple bounces among the fibres and leaves the back face a centimetre away, so a bright patch really does lend part of itself to the dark cloth beside it. That is what makes one light patch read as a hard spotlight and the next as glowing haze — the contrast the tent photograph above is *about*, and the thing no per-pixel term can produce. `glow_bleed = d ∈ [0,1]` is, once again, a **split and never an add**:

> `out = mix(sharp, blur(sharp), d)`,  `blur` = a Gaussian of radius `glow_bleed_m` **metres of cloth**

The energy bound is now exact rather than merely respected: a normalized kernel *moves* light, it cannot make any, so what the hot patch loses is precisely what its surround gains and the frame's total is unchanged. `d = 0` is the gate.

**Why this one costs — the buffer, and where the tail sits.** A neighbourhood average of *display-encoded* pixels is not an average of light — the tone curve is steeply non-linear exactly where the glow lives (the hot dapples sit on its shoulder), so a blur applied after it spreads the wrong quantity and throws away the very highlight energy the effect exists to redistribute. So with the tier on, transport writes **linear HDR into an RGBA16F target** and the **Look's tail sits past the blur**: exposure → tone curve → contrast → gamma run in the compositing pass. What stays *upstream*, deliberately, is everything perceptual and atmospheric — the mesopic shift and the distance haze — so the diffusion acts on **post-mesopic** linear colour. Physically the fabric spreads the light before any eye adapts to it, so that ordering is inverted; recorded as an honest approximation rather than hidden, and negligible in practice, since the mesopic term is gated to deep shade at a low sun while the glow is carried by the hot patches — the two barely overlap. The tail itself lives as **one GLSL string included by both shaders**, not two copies that happen to agree: with two paths now running the same Look, shared source is the only thing that keeps the gate's off state honest a year from now.

**The radius is cloth, not pixels.** The receiver is viewed head-on through a linear map (`cloth = (uv − ½)·view_extent_m·(aspect, 1)`), so screen space **is** cloth space up to one isotropic scale — a screen-space blur here is a genuine cloth-space diffusion, with no reprojection, and that is a property of the standing curtain the floor does not have. px/m is therefore one number, the target's height over `view_extent_m`, and the bleed stays the same centimetres of fabric at any zoom or backing-store size. What the blur does *not* know is the corrugation: it spreads across the flat cloth coordinate, not along the pleated surface, so a fold's extra path length never lengthens the bleed — sub-percent at any real `fold_warp`, and stated for completeness.

**The kernel, and exactly where it stops being honest.** Separable H then V, 13 taps each (13 + 13, not 169), σ = radius/3 so the taps span ±3σ — which makes the **weights constant** (`exp(−k²/8)`, normalized) and puts the whole radius into the tap *spacing*. Thirteen taps cannot cover an arbitrarily large radius, and this is the one place the feature is knowingly wrong: past a capped spacing the frame is sampled too sparsely, and a hard cast edge would come back as thirteen shifted copies of itself instead of a ramp — so the spacing caps at 6 px and the **reach then falls short of the metres asked for** (the cap bites above a 36 px radius — roughly 7 cm at the tent's 3 m framing on a 1600 px-tall canvas, and *more* cloth than that on a shorter one, since the same 36 px then span more metres). The trade is deliberate and the direction is chosen: a glow that stops short still reads as a glow, a combed one reads as a bug. The next rung, if reach ever matters more than pass count, is the standard one — a downsampled pyramid, where each level's box average is the pre-filter that makes a sparse kernel legal in the first place.

**Where the passes sit.** Transport → H → V → composite, and all four are **heavy work**: under the adaptive frame-rate (§9) they run together on the idle cadence and the cheap re-present blit shows the composited frame, exactly as it showed transport's before. Off — `receiver = 0`, or `d = 0` — none of it exists: no target is allocated, no pass is issued, and transport draws straight to the screen through the same branch it always did (a driver that refuses a complete 16F target falls silently back to that same path). Per §9: the gate is `glow_bleed`, the declared fallback rung is the forward-scatter wrap and then `s = 0`, the profiler carries it as a **style** axis beside diffraction — measured for its price, *never* auto-cut — and the only thing that may cross it is an author, on one look.

**The enclosure (the receiver stands around you).** §1's tent reference is not a photograph *of* a piece of fabric; it is one taken from **inside** one. Canopy overhead, light falling **down** onto panels that surround the viewer — and the detail that carries the whole read: *different panels at different brightnesses, because they face different ways*. No flat receiver can produce that: **a plane has one normal**, so every point of it meets the beam at the identical angle, and no amount of exposure, drape, sun-angle or distance work can make one wall read as being *inside* anything. So `receiver = 2` builds the enclosure itself — the first receiver whose surface orientation is a function of the pixel.

**The shape is the reference's, decomposed by the person lying in it**: **(1)** a flat rectangular **crown** panel running along the top; **(2)** **left and right side walls** rising to the crown's two long edges; **(3)** a **far gable** that *closes* the tent — the panel the space converges onto. *Do not build the enclosure as an infinite prism:* an infinite ridge converges to a **vanishing point**, and a real enclosure converges to **flatness** — a surface at a finite distance with an edge you can see. The eye reads "endless corridor" versus "small room I am inside of" from exactly that, and no amount of distance fade standing in for a wall will produce the second. *A depth cue is not a boundary; only a boundary is a boundary.*

**The cross-section is a bell, not a wedge.** The end-on views show a wide **skirt** rising from the base to a **bulged shoulder** at about half height, above which the wall leans back **in** to the narrow crown — the bulge, the shoulder standing *outside* the straight base→crown line, is what makes a modern tent feel like a room. It costs one more plane per side, which is the whole argument for the polytope: shape here is rows in a plane table, not structure.

*Two constraints keep the tent from reading as a church.* Flat panels meeting at angles, with a dark rib drawn along each meeting, is the grammar of a stone vault — and both halves must be refused. First, the reference is **tensioned fabric on pre-bent pole arcs**: its cross-section is a rounded arch with no crease along the side at all — the shoulder is where the arch bulges widest, not a fold. Second, a seam pass that cannot tell a **modelling artefact** from a **real edge** faithfully draws the artefacts: once a curve is approximated by facets, every facet junction is a lie the renderer is otherwise free to hide. The rule outlives tents: *when a smooth thing is approximated by pieces, every downstream pass that reads the pieces must be told which boundaries are the object's and which are the approximation's.* Shading normals have known that for fifty years; edge-drawing passes need telling too.

**The sides are arches.** Each side is one **arch**: a quadratic Bézier through the same three authored control points — base `(halfW, 0)`, the shoulder waypoint `(shoulderW, shoulderH)` hit exactly at `t = 0.5`, crown edge `(crownW, ridge)` — which pins the control point at `C = 2·P1 − (P0+P2)/2`. A quadratic Bézier is a parabola arc, so its curvature cannot change sign and the curve is **convex by construction**; and the tangent planes of a convex curve are a convex half-space set. That is what lets the shape round for free: sampling four tangent planes per side buys an arch at the cost of four more rows in the plane table, and the ray-exit machinery below is untouched. Tangency is taken at `t = 0, ⅓, ⅔, 1` — the ends **included**, so the hull touches the authored base and crown edge exactly and only bulges between them (measured: at most **1.45 cm** outside the true arc at the shipped dimensions). The shoulder parameters describe where the vault bulges widest.

**Smooth normals over a coarse hull.** The shading normal on an arch is the **profile's own**, evaluated at the hit's height by inverting the Bézier's height map — not the facet's — so `beamF`, `skyF` and the cloth tangent round *continuously* across the side the way light rounds on tensioned fabric. Measured across an intra-arch facet boundary the normal moves by `4·10⁻⁶`; across a real pole line it jumps by 0.8, which is what a dihedral should do. This is the classic **smooth-shaded coarse hull**, and it is the honest cheap road here rather than a shortcut being excused: a true curved-surface ray intersection would buy sub-centimetre silhouette accuracy on a tent 64 cm wide — invisible — and would cost the closed-form single `min()` this whole receiver is built on. The silhouette stays subtly faceted at four strips; the *lighting*, which is what the eye reads as roundness, does not.

**The hubs need no modelling, and that is the argument.** The corners the reference reads as hardware are simply **vertices of the polytope** — where an arch strip, the crown and a cap plane meet — so they land in the right place for free, and the seam pass below draws the pole lines radiating out of them without a single edge being enumerated. A mesh-and-vertex approach would have had to author them; a half-space intersection *derives* them. Panel count is data here, not structure.

**The geometry: a convex polytope, and one min().** In tent space the long axis is +Y. Thirteen half-spaces, each an outward unit normal `n̂` and an offset `c`, with the interior at `n̂·X < c`:

> CROWN `(0,0,1)·X < ridge` · ARCH strips, four per side, tangent to the profile at `t = 0, ⅓, ⅔, 1`: `n̂ = (B'ᶻ, −B'ˣ)/‖·‖` through `B(t)`, mirrored to `−x` by negating `n̂.x` at the same offset · EACH CAP, three members of one family `(a·x ± y + k·z)/‖·‖ < (L + rake·apex)/‖·‖` with `a = ±apex·rake/halfW` (the two hips) or `a = rake = 0` (the end wall), `k = lean + rake`, `L = len` far / `½` near and the `y` sign flipping with the end

The caps lean **out** as they descend, the far one reaching its base at `y = tent_len_m`, the near one mirrored half a metre behind the origin so that turning to look back also closes. The eye sits at `y = 0.3·tent_len_m` — **derived, not a knob**: 30 % down the tent is where you lie in a 2P.

**Each end is a hip cap, and its three planes are one family.** Lying with your feet toward the far end, the seam runs away from you down the middle of the ceiling, forks just short of the end, and the two halves drop apart to the floor corners — and in the crotch of that fork sits the little bright triangle of the vent, apex up, its point exactly where the seam divided. Roll your head back and the same thing is happening behind you. A cap is a small upright **vent triangle** standing on the floor with two **hip** panels sloping up off its apex to the ceiling. All three of its planes are members of

> `a·x ± y + k·z < L + rake·apex`,  with  `a = apex·rake/halfW`,  `k = tent_end_lean + rake`,  `apex = tent_end_apex_h_m`,  `rake = ±tent_hip_rake` or `0`,  and `L = tent_len_m` for the far end or `½` for the near one, the `y` sign flipping with it

`rake = 0` gives `a = 0`, `k = tent_end_lean` and offset `L`: a single leaning end wall. `+rake` and `−rake` are the two hips. **The two ends are the same six numbers with one sign flipped**, so a shape statement made about one is a statement about both; the near cap's base line sits at `y = −½` and the far one's at `y = tent_len_m`, with the eye between them at `0.3·tent_len_m`. Carry a plane family's variants on the parent's **offset** and the family collapses onto the parent *exactly* rather than nearly — measured here, `0` difference in normal and in offset at `rake = 0`, and a `257²` frame in which no pixel's winning panel or exit distance differs from the single-wall shape.

The cap's shape follows from that one form. The two hips differ only in the sign of `a`, so **they meet exactly on `x = 0`**: their junction is the ceiling's centreline seam, the **spine**. All three members pass through the apex `A = (0, ±(L − tent_end_lean·apex), apex)`, the point on that end wall at the vent's height, so the spine terminates there and the two hip↔wall junctions start there. Each hip passes through its own floor corner `(±halfW, ±L, 0)`, which is what pins `a`; the hip↔wall junction is therefore the straight line from the apex down to the tent's own far corner, and the end wall survives **exactly** on the triangle `|x|/halfW + z/apex < 1` — apex up, base on the floor. The triangle is not authored: it is what the three planes leave. Measured at the shipped dimensions with the far end in frame, its visible pixel region is `93` px wide at its base row and narrows **monotonically** to `1` px at its apex row, centred to the pixel on the frame's centre column.

**`tent_hip_rake` is the hips' excess lean over the wall's, and it is floored at `0.15` rather than gated at zero.** A hip binds only where it rakes back *faster* than the wall does, so below the floor the cap has no hips at all and the shape silently reverts to the slab. The seam is safe at any rake — the edge-line measure divides by `sin(dihedral)`, which goes to zero exactly as the three planes converge, so a degenerate cap draws no seam rather than painting itself dark. The floor is therefore about **shape**, not about the seam pass; at `0.15` the setback is ≈ 4 cm at the shipped dimensions.

*Do not model a converging seam pattern as a fold.* A tent's foot end shows two seams converging to a point, and that point has a **height** — it is where the spine meets the vent apex. A yaw about the vertical axis slides a crease sideways and gives it no height to converge on, so no setting of such a knob draws the pattern. Where a reference shows lines converging, the free parameter is *where* they converge, not which way they lean.

**The enclosure's yaw is a lighting control and a framing control at once, and the two are not separable.** The eye is fixed on the tent's centreline and only the gaze turns, so `view_yaw_deg` sets both which panels are in frame and — since it turns the tent's axes against the world sun — each panel's `beamF`. At `view_yaw_deg −115.34` and the shipped dimensions the frame is 35 % the `−x` arch above its hem, 40 % the two near hips, 14 % that arch below the hem and 11 % crown; at `view_yaw_deg 0` the same tent reads 33/33 % arches, 14 % crown, 18 % the two far hips and 0.7 % the foot triangle.

**A vent triangle is centred on its own end wall, so only a gaze pointed near that end contains it.** Its centreline lies at azimuth 0° (far) or 180° (near) in tent space, and no shape knob moves that: `tent_end_apex_h_m` widens the triangle about its centreline but cannot slide it sideways. At the shipped framing the gaze centre sits 64.7° off the near end against a horizontal half-width of 59.8°, so the near vent's nearest corner falls **3.3° outside the left edge** — the frame holds the near hips and the spine between them, and not the triangle they converge on. Raising the apex does eventually widen the triangle into frame (0.65 m on 16:9), but the value needed **depends on the canvas aspect**: 0.75 m at 16:10, 0.85 m at 4:3, and unreachable within the clamp at 1:1. *A shape tuned until a feature scrapes the edge of one canvas is undone by a window resize; where a feature is out of frame, the camera is the control that works.* Measured: `view_yaw_deg −130` puts the near vent at 2.6 % of frame, `view_fov_deg 100` at 0.9 %.

**The pitch bounds what is visible below the eye.** At `view_pitch_deg 42.5` with an 88° lens the frame's lowest ray is depressed ≈ 1.5°, so from an eye 0.42 m up nothing below eye height is in shot: a vent triangle's base on the floor lies outside the frame, and what reaches the picture is its upper third, cut by the frame edge rather than by the ground. Where a vent is in frame the shape is what it should be — at `view_yaw_deg 180` the near triangle occupies 123 rows of an 801-row frame, **125 px wide at its base row narrowing monotonically to 1 px at its apex**, centred to the pixel on the frame's centre column.

Convexity is what makes the intersection both exact and cheap: from a point **strictly inside**, a ray leaves through exactly one panel, so the exit is a bare

> `t = min over planes with n̂·d > 0 of (c − n̂·eye)/(n̂·d)`,  panel = the argmin

with **no per-panel bounds test anywhere** — a plane the ray meets outside the tent is always beaten by the one that actually bounds it there. That is the entire routine; it holds for any panel count, and it is why the shape grew two planes → five → nine → thirteen at the cost of the planes alone. Measured on a 129×129 ray grid at the shipped dimensions: **zero rays with no exit plane, zero clamped at `TENT_TMAX`**, exit distances 0.61–1.35 m, frame coverage with the far end in shot **33 % / 33 % the two arches, 14 % crown, 9.2 % / 8.9 % the two hips, 0.7 % the foot triangle**. Across dense screen sweeps (240 000 samples per line) the hit point's jump at **every** panel→panel crossing is `≤ 1.5·10⁻⁵ m`, **smaller than the step between ordinary neighbouring samples** on the same sweep: continuous, no gap, no overlap. `TENT_TMAX` survives only as a safety clamp for the single exitless direction, straight down out of the open bottom the model has no floor for, which no frame at a sane pitch contains.

**Two invariants, both CPU-side, and one of them is subtler than it looks.** The first is that **the eye sits strictly inside** every half-space: that is what makes every numerator positive and lets the min() skip all sign handling, and it is enforced by clamping the eye under the crown, inside both end walls, **and inside both hips** — each raking back faster than its own wall, so it overtakes that wall as the binding constraint the moment the eye rides above the vent apex. The near pair binds first, being the closer cap. All four are taken as one `min` rather than resolved case by case: over-tightening the eye costs nothing, letting it out of the tent costs the whole routine. The side walls never bind at `x = 0`, and neither hip's `x` term does either. The second is that **the arch bows outward and stays single-valued in height**. On the first half it is worth stating what is *not* being protected: an intersection of half-spaces is convex whatever the planes do, so the min-exit is never wrong. What breaks is the **shape** — put the shoulder *inside* the straight base→crown line and the arc bends the wrong way, so the lower tangent planes stop containing the crown edge and slice it off; push it far enough and the eye itself ends up outside, which is the first invariant. The second half arrived with the curve: the profile's height sweep is monotone exactly when the Bézier's control point lies strictly between the ends in `z`, i.e. `tent_shoulder_h_m ∈ (0.25, 0.75)·ridge`. Outside that the arc doubles back — the tangent's rise changes sign, the height inversion the smooth normal depends on has two answers, and `normalize` is handed a zero vector. It is clamped to `[0.30, 0.70]·ridge`, a margin inside. *(The eye-inside proof gets simpler, not harder, from the rounding: tangent planes **circumscribe** a convex curve, so the hull is a superset of the true arch, and an eye inside the arch is inside the hull for free.)*

The shape **contains its predecessors**, and those are the regression anchors: placing the shoulder exactly *on* the straight line makes all three Bézier control points collinear, so the curve *is* that line and all four tangent strips collapse to one plane — measured, the four differ by `1·10⁻¹⁶` in normal and exactly `0` in offset, which is the five-panel tent's flat slope; adding `tent_crown_w_m = 0` and a large `tent_len_m` on top of that gives back the infinite A-frame.

Then the hit's plan point (rotated by `view_yaw_deg`, offset by `view_center`, so the tent stands wherever the look places it) and its height go into `world` and `recvZ` — **and nothing downstream changes**. The layer loop's `(layerHeight − recvZ)·g` read and the analytic woody occluder cast the canopy onto whichever panel the pixel landed on with no further work at all. That generalization was written in "The standing plane" above for a *flat vertical cloth*, and the enclosure is the proof of what it actually was: the read is **geometry-free**, valid for any receiver that can name a plan point and a height per pixel. The curtain was one plane; this is one of thirteen, chosen per pixel; the transport code is character-for-character the same.

**The layer tier is the general-geometry tier; the faithful tier is the flat-plane one.** The faithful bake pre-integrates the entire disk convolution into **one texture in one frame** — floor `(x,y)` or cloth `(u,v)` — which is exactly why it is a single tap, and exactly why it cannot serve here: the enclosure's receiver is neither of those frames, and no single frame exists for it. So `receiver = 2` **ignores `faithful_canopy`** and forces the layer path, in the shader and on the CPU alike (one derived predicate, `faithfulOn()`, gates the bake dispatch, the texture sizing, the per-mode cluster packing and the quality model together — they must agree per frame or the layer bake reads faithful-packed data and the leaves slide off their twigs). This is not the loss it would be on the floor. At enclosure distances the whole cast is deep in the **pinhole regime** — metres of occluder height above fabric a metre or two off the ground — where the disk blur is centimetres wide and the layer tier's height quantization is invisible underneath it. §3.2 decides which tier is honest, and here it says the cheap path *is* the correct path. Recorded as the general rule: **the pre-baked tier buys speed by fixing a receiver frame, and buys it only where a receiver frame exists.**

**Per-panel light — the first time orientation shapes the light.** With a normal per pixel, the landed irradiance finally has to resolve the beam and the sky **separately**, because they are different kinds of thing: the beam is a direction and takes an incidence cosine, the sky is a hemisphere and takes how much of that hemisphere the panel can see.

> `E = acc·sunColor·beamF + ambient·skyF`,  `beamF = clamp(n̂·sunDir / max(sunDir.z, 0.15), 0, 2)`,  `skyF = 0.5 + 0.5·n̂.z`

The normalization is the load-bearing part, and the **crown is what anchors it**. Every earlier receiver's irradiance convention **already absorbs `sin(elevation)`** — `acc` is the fraction of the sun's disk that cleared the canopy, landing on a surface the floor and curtain models never tilt — so "as bright as the old receivers" has to mean **exactly 1 for a horizontal panel**, and dividing by the sun's own vertical component is what delivers that at any elevation. The crown *is* that horizontal panel: it lands at `beamF = 1.000000` from 90° down to the 0.15 divisor floor at ≈8.6° (below which the term is deliberately capped rather than allowed to diverge at a horizon sun), and every other panel reads as a departure from it. It is the same discipline as the fold incidence above — an incidence ratio normalized so the untilted case is unity and the knob is the geometry — one dimension up: there the flat cloth was the reference, here the crown is. The cap at 2 is the ceiling on a panel turned squarely into a low sun, which really does collect more than a horizontal one.

The consequence is the picture, and the arch turns it from a set of steps into a gradient. Because the shading normal follows the profile rather than the facet, `beamF` and `skyF` vary **continuously down each side** — the light rolls off the vault the way it rolls off tensioned fabric, brightest where the arch turns into the sun and darkening smoothly as it turns away, with no banding at the strip boundaries. What remains a *step* is exactly what should be one: the crown against the arches, and each cap panel against both. That is the payoff for modelling the shape rather than painting a gradient — an edge is not a line you draw, it is the boundary between two irradiances, and a place with no edge has no boundary. The two sides are equal **only** when the sun lies in the tent's own long vertical plane (`sunDir.x = 0`), and it is worth recording which azimuths those are, because the intuition points the wrong way: the engine's sun vector is `(cos el·cos az, cos el·sin az, sin el)`, so with the long axis along +Y the axis-aligned azimuths are **90° and 270°** — `az = 0/180` is the sun standing *square to* the tent, the maximum-contrast case, not the minimum. `skyF` is common to a mirrored pair (`n̂.z` is), which is right: a hemisphere of sky has no azimuth to favour.

**Depth, and the seams.** Two more terms multiply `E`, both enclosure-only. `exp(−t·tent_fade)` is now a **gentle** depth cue rather than the thing that closes the space — the far cap does that, and the tent is metres deep, not infinite — so at the 0.06/m default it is a few percent across the frame, keeping the near fabric a touch brighter than the far. And the **seams**, which are now **only the pole lines**: the crown's two long edges running away from you, the **spine** down the ceiling's centreline where the two hips meet, the **vent triangle's two rising sides** where each hip meets the end wall, and the rims where a cap meets the arches and the crown. They are near-contact occluders in the strictest possible sense, since they are *on* the cloth: they take beam and sky alike, exactly as the mullion bars do and for the same hemisphere argument. All of them come from **one** measurement — the inside-distance to each non-winning plane **of a different panel group**, minimised:

> `E ← E · exp(−tent_seam · (1 − smoothstep(hw, hw + pen, min_{group(j) ≠ group(win)} d_j)))`,  `d_j = |c_j − n̂_j·hit| / √(1 − (n̂_j·n̂_win)²)`,  `hw = 12 mm`,  `pen = max(hw, 0.004·t)`

The **group filter is the whole difference between a tent and a vault.** Every plane carries a group id — `{crown, arch-left below/above the hem, arch-right below/above, and per end: end wall, hip +x, hip −x}` — and a junction between two planes of the *same* group is an artefact of faceting a curve, not an edge the object has. Skipping those is what lets the arch be four planes and still read as one continuous piece of fabric. Measured: of 107 388 arch pixels 23 468 sit on an intra-arch facet boundary, and their distance to the nearest cross-group plane runs 0.078–0.408 m (median 0.173) against the 12 mm core — 0.64 % of them land in a core, and those are the **hub vertices**, where a real cap rim genuinely crosses the facet boundary; 1.8 % of all arch pixels seam, every one a crown edge or a cap rim. **Each cap's three planes are three separate groups**, because their junctions are edges the object has: the spine where the hips meet, and the vent triangle's two rising sides. With both ends hipped the spine is continuous along the ceiling's whole centreline: it comes in over your head from behind, crosses the crown, and forks again at the far vent. The group filter cannot hide a junction that is meant to draw, which is why the rake floor above is geometric rather than a grouping. **The distance is to the EDGE LINE, not to the neighbouring plane**, and that is the difference between a tape and a stain. A plane's perpendicular distance is the distance to the edge foreshortened by the dihedral — crossing the winning panel toward the junction closes that gap at a rate `sin(dihedral)` — so a plane-distance seam paints a band `1/sin(dihedral)` wide **in fabric**, and the shallowest junction draws the fattest tape. One identity undoes it: split the neighbour's normal into its component along the winner's and the rest, and only the rest carries you toward the line, so `d_j = |c_j − n̂_j·hit| · (1 − (n̂_j·n̂_win)²)^(−½)`. One dot and one `inversesqrt` per plane. *A distance to a plane is not a distance to that plane's edge, and any pass that paints a band around an edge has to say which it means.* Measured at the shipped dimensions: the mesh hem, at 18.4° the shallowest dihedral in the tent, painted **76.0 mm** of fabric and now paints **24.0 mm**; a crown edge at 46.9° painted 32.8 mm and now paints 24.0 mm — both exactly `hw + pen`, and now equal, which is what a taped seam is. The `1e-6` floor under the radical is not a numerical nicety: two **parallel** panels have no edge, the distance to it is infinite, and no seam is drawn. The soft edge is the tape's own near to and the **pixel footprint** far off (0.004 rad ≈ 3 px on a 78°, 1080-tall frame), because a junction receding from the eye thins to a sub-pixel line and a sub-pixel dark line crawls. `tent_seam = 0` is the gate.

**The mesh band — material per panel, not per surface.** The tent's sides are two materials with a hard hem between them: solid nylon from the floor to the shoulder, insect mesh above it. `tent_mesh ∈ [0,1]` is how far the upper panels are mesh, and it splits the transmission in **both** of its halves, because those are the two things a mesh does differently from a weave:

> upper panels: `body ← body · mix(1, 0.55, tent_mesh)`  and  `scatter ← fabric_scatter · (1 − tent_mesh)`

A mesh passes less of what lands on it — the throughput term — and **diffuses almost none of what it does pass** — the forward-scatter share, which is the term that turns a cast pattern into a glow. Taking both is what makes the band read *darker and crisper at the same time*, which is the pair of cues that separates mesh from nylon by eye. `tent_mesh = 0` leaves both expressions at their old values exactly, and the term is `0` on every other panel and every other receiver, so this is the receiver interface swapping material **per face** rather than per surface — the axis the whole-surface swap (floor ↔ curtain ↔ enclosure) does not reach.

**The hem is a panel-group boundary, and the shading normal does not know about it.** Each arch's four tangent strips split into a lower group (`t = 0, ⅓`) and an upper group (`t = ⅔, 1`); the seam pass, which draws only cross-group junctions, therefore draws exactly one line along each side. At the shipped dimensions that line lands at `z = 0.5533 m`, 0.33 cm above the authored shoulder waypoint — the strip junction is where the arc's tangents cross, which is the widest point of the vault, which is where a real tent's hem sits. Across the hem the *shading* normal moves by `1.9·10⁻⁴` while the two facet normals differ by `0.32`: the fabric changes material there, not direction, so the light still rolls continuously around the curve through the band. The hem is the **shallowest dihedral in the polytope** at 18.4°, which is why the seam pass measures to the edge line rather than to the neighbouring plane: measured the other way it would paint 76 mm of fabric here against 33 mm at a crown edge, and a hem three times the width of a pole line reads as a stain rather than a binding. *A material boundary and a geometric edge are different things: do not let one stand in for the other.* A crease at the shoulder would produce a brightness step that reads like the hem, but the reference has no crease there — it is tensioned fabric on a bent pole — so the hem must be carried by material and by the group filter, and it survives the side being perfectly smooth.

**Where this leads.** A mesh panel is not a cast surface at all — it is a window onto the canopy — so the honest end of this axis is for an upper panel to run the *sky view*'s transport (§4.9's look-up branch) instead of the fabric's. `tent_mesh` is the one-sided approximation of that: it darkens and de-diffuses the band, which is what a mesh looks like from inside at midday, but no light reaches the eye through it that did not first land on it.


**The fabric is the curtain's, unchanged.** `fabric_tt`, the dye, the forward-scatter split and the lateral diffusion are the same cloth physics on the same code — the enclosure changes the *geometry* the light arrives on, not the material that answers it, which is the §4.9 interface doing precisely what it was promoted for. The cloth coordinate generalizes off the crown: `v` is the drop **below the crown plane**, so the pleat field's rod sits on the tent's own pinned top on every panel at once (a tent is pinned at its crown and free at the hem exactly as a curtain is at its rod, so `fold_coarsen`'s octave crossfade broadens the ripples downward the right way round), and `u` runs along each panel's horizontal tangent — down the tent on the slopes, across it on the cap panels, and across it on the crown, whose plan normal is degenerate and takes an explicit fallback. What is **curtain-only** and switches itself off here: `cloth_distance_m` (world-Y comes out of the ray cast now), and the window aperture and mullion grid, which are cast by a central-ray throw against a plane at world-Y `cy` — an object the enclosure does not have, in coordinates that would mean nothing on a panel.

Honest approximations, stated: **the hull is faceted, the shading is not.** The sides are a true convex arc sampled as four tangent strips, so the *silhouette* is a thirteen-plane polytope standing at most 1.45 cm outside the real curve while the *lighting* rounds continuously; a genuine curved-surface intersection would close that centimetre and cost the closed-form `min()`, which is not a trade worth making at tent scale. The road onward, if it ever matters, is more strips — not a different method. The caps and the crown are still genuinely flat, which the reference mostly supports. **Both ends carry the same cap**, which a 2P does not: its head end is a door and its foot end a vent. Modelling the door is future work, and until then the near end is the foot end mirrored. The door, the vestibule, the poles themselves and the guy lines are all future work. The mesh band above is **material** per panel, not transport per panel: an upper panel is darkened and stripped of its forward scatter, but the light reaching the eye through it still had to land on it first. A true mesh panel is a window rather than a receiver, and reading the canopy through one means running the look-up branch's transport on that face — which is where the enclosure and the sky view meet, and is not built. There is **no interreflection**: the shaded slope is lit only by its own sky share, where in a real white tent a large part of its light has bounced off the lit slope opposite — that missing bounce is the physical reason the shaded side wants lifting, and the honest fix is a bounce term, not a bigger ambient (recorded, not built). There is **no floor**, so the polytope is open below and a straight-down ray is the one direction with no exit. `fold_warp` is **inert** in this branch — it slides a read along a flat cast plane, and there is no flat cast plane here; the shading half (`fold_depth`, the sheen, the nap) still applies. And the lateral-diffusion tier's px/m conversion still divides by `view_extent_m`, which is the head-on cloth map's scale and no longer the enclosure's: the blur radius there is **approximate**, uniform in screen space across fabric that is receding. Left as-is deliberately — it is a screen-space post pass by construction, and making it depth-aware is a different tier, not a fix to this one.

**One designed step is staged, not built**, gated off so the floor and every existing look stay byte-identical:

- **A fuller pile — the directional nap** — deliberately still deferred now that the warp has landed. A real cut pile is *brushed*: the fibres lean, and that one field should drive **both** the sheen lobe in reflection **and** a transmission asymmetry, so the grain you see and the glow you see through agree instead of being two unrelated textures. What ships today is the shading sheen plus a fine `nap` ripple keyed to the pleat scale — a grain, not a lean. The lean needs a direction field, and it is not built. *(The ripple varies across `u`, at an irrational multiple of `fold_scale` so it never locks onto a fold, at ±4 % — a `v`-varying ripple draws horizontal etched stripes, the opposite of a vertical pile grain, and past ±5 % a grain reads as engraving.)*

The governing intent is unchanged: **model reality, not the artifacts.** The curtain glows because the light really passes through dyed pile and the window really blocks its shape — never because a glow was painted on.

### The sky view — the canopy seen against the source (`canopy 1`)

**The second and third amendments to "we never see the tree".** §2's fourth principle said we never see the tree, only what it throws on the ground; §4.8 amended it once, when a backlit scene made the trunk part of what reaches the surface. `sky_view` amends it a second time and further: **the surface the light reaches is now the retina.** And a third time, which reverses it outright: **in this view we should see the tree**, and what reaches the eye has to read as an object rather than as a pattern that happens to have a tree behind it. That sets three requirements the rest of this section answers — the wood must be **connected** trunk to limb to twig to leaf, it must **taper** by the pipe model as it goes, and it must be **dark**, since an occluder brighter than what it occludes is a light and not a body. What the amendment does not yet buy: no lit bark, no sun on a trunk's flank, no visible-surface shading. The tree here is an object made of shadow, correct in structure and unlit; the shaded body is the direction and is not built. The reference is a hammock at a festival — lying back, tall trunks converging overhead, crowns lit from behind against the bright sky, the whole grove leaning together with the gusts. This closes §1's loop, because it is not a new scene at all: it is **the founding afternoon with the eyes turned the other way**, the same grove and the same sun, no longer read off the floor but looked at.

**It invents nothing.** That is the point, and it is why the whole feature is one branch in the camera chain and no new physics. The engine has always had an occluder field of per-channel optical depth at known heights, and a source with a known angular distribution. The floor path integrates that field along the **sun's** rays and asks what fraction of the disk survives; this integrates the *same* field along the **eye's** rays and asks what the eye receives. Pure transmission either way:

> `col = T · (sky + L_source(θ))`,  `T` = Π over layers of `e^(−τ(p_L))` × the wood's `e^(−τ·coverage)`

The ray crosses layer `L`'s plane at plan `eye + dir.xy·(h_L/dir.z)` — the same texture, the same tap, and deliberately **no shift by `g`**: `g` is where the *sun's* rays land, and this is the eye's. No `recvZ` either, since these are absolute heights over an eye on the ground. **Per-channel Beer's law then arrives for free**: `tap()` exponentiates a per-channel optical depth, so a leaf between the eye and the sun passes its own green-gold and the canopy *glows* rather than silhouetting flat. §4.5's leaf transmittance, which every other look infers from a dapple's colour, is finally seen head-on. Outside the baked canopy box the read returns 1 — beyond the grove there is open sky, and an upward frame leaves that box within metres, so the alternative is a clamped border texel smeared across the whole periphery.

**Wood as silhouette — the same segments, read as geometry.** The trunks and main limbs (§4.5's analytic occluder) are the reference's defining structure, and they need no new data: the same `uOccSeg`/`uOccHt` the floor casts shadows with, minus the one term that makes it a shadow. There is **no `uBulkShift`** here — that vector is where the wood's shadow *falls*, and we are looking at the wood itself. The sway is kept, each endpoint drifting by its own height fraction exactly as the cast does, which is what puts §5.1's coherent band on screen **directly** instead of inferred from a moving floor pattern: the unison lean of a whole grove is the look's soul and it comes from the motion system untouched. Geometrically it is the closest approach between the view ray and each segment — a clamped ray/segment minimisation in closed form, verified against dense sampling to `3·10⁻¹⁰ m` — and the columns converge on the zenith by **projection alone**. Nothing draws a trunk.

**The occluder budget is a composition constraint here, not a performance one.** The analytic occluder is a fixed uniform array of 64 segments, filled in grow order with every level ≤ 1 segment until it runs out: one **bole** per tree plus one per **level-1 limb** (more if `|droop| ≥ 0.3`, which splits a limb into sub-segments). On the floor a truncated tail costs a few branch shadows. In this camera the wood **is** the structure, so a grove past the budget loses whole trees' trunks — the later ones simply have none. A look whose subject is trunks therefore trades limbs against trees: `tree_count·(limb_count + 1) ≤ 64` is the rule, and it is why the canopy looks carry limb counts a floor look would call sparse.

**The source, seen.** §3.1 said the sun is not a point and §3.2 said every dapple is its image; this is the first time the thing being imaged is in the frame, and it must be the *same* thing. Both regions come straight from the sampler: a core disk of `core_angular_radius_deg` and a halo annulus out to the cloud-widened `halo_angular_radius_deg`, each at the radiance its own **post-cloud weight over its own solid angle** gives. So `cloud_thickness` drains the disk into the aureole *here* by exactly the amount it softens the dapples *there* — measured, the seen field's core:halo energy split matches the sampler's weights to **0.08 %** across clear, hazy and overcast. The annulus is flat rather than a pretty falloff **because the sampler's is flat**: a smooth glow would look better and would no longer be the source the cast is drawn from. And the **eclipse** carries over whole — the same moon disk the sampler punches out of its weights, applied angularly in the same source plane (reconstructed from `uProj`'s eigenvectors, so the crescent is at the *same orientation* the dapples image it at). Looking up during `eclipse`, you see the crescent itself, through the leaves, throwing crescents.

**Two authored scales, and why they cannot be physical.** A sun disk is about `10⁶` times a blue sky's radiance. No tone curve and no 13-tap glare kernel carries that, so the absolute levels are authored and stated as such rather than dressed up: `SKY_SUN_GAIN` multiplies the sampler's angular density (leaving the core:halo ratio exactly the sampler's), and `SKY_SKY_GAIN` converts `uAmbient` — an authored **irradiance**, what the sky delivers *onto* a surface — into the **radiance** you get looking straight at it, which is a different quantity the engine never had. `ambient_skylight` still steers the sky, and `SKY_SKY_GAIN` is the one control that lifts the sky **without** lifting the canopy — it multiplies the radiance seen looking at the sky and never the irradiance landing on a leaf, so raising it moves open sky above the brightest crown rather than taking both up together. At the shipped daylight setting open sky lands at sRGB (184, 208, 232), luminance 205 against the thinnest sunlit crown's 197, and the disk clips white.

**The veiling glare is part of the light, not a decoration on top of it.** `glow_bleed` mixes a blurred copy of the frame in **linear HDR before the tone curve** (§4.9), so a sky pixel's rendered value falls between `(1−d)·sky` and `sky` depending on how much dark canopy its neighbourhood holds — a wide setting drags open sky down toward the canopy's own mean and reads as haze over the whole picture. At `d = 0.35` it costs a daylight sky 13 sRGB levels; the same term over a dark preset takes (81,109,157) to (68,93,138). Set it to halo the source and nothing else. Recorded as the honest description: *a compressed stand-in for a contrast the pipeline cannot carry*, the same class of deliberate non-physicality as `window_wall`.

**Veiling glare — the same pass, read one tier out.** §4.9's lateral diffusion spreads light through a weave; in the sky view the *identical* pass over the *identical* linear-HDR frame reads as the eye's own **veiling glare**, the wash that grows around a bright source seen through gaps. The scattering has moved from the fabric to the observer's eye, but it is the same operator on the same quantity, so it is the same code with its gate widened. That is a **perception-tier** reading of a transport pass — the same honesty class as the mesopic shift, which likewise models the eye rather than the scene. The mesopic hook needed nothing at all: it is cued by `acc`, and `acc` in this branch is the ray's own transmittance — still exactly "how much of the light got through here".

**The canopy glows: single-scatter foliage radiance.** Look up through a grove on a clear day and the picture is mostly *bright*. The sky is the ground tone, and the crowns with sun on them are luminous green-gold — brighter than the crowns beside them in shade, and lit from within rather than merely thinner. That is the whole difference between a canopy and a stencil, and pure transmission cannot produce it: `T·(sky + source)` only ever removes light, so every leaf is a dim filter and a deep crown is black.

Light reaches a leaf, scatters inside it and leaves in every direction. Along the eye's ray that is a **source term**, and over a layered occluder field it is a sum rather than an integral — one term per layer, accumulated **eye → sky**:

> `L += throughput·(1 − t_L)·ŝ_L·S_L`,  `throughput *= t_L`,  and  `col = T·(sky + L_source) + sky_scatter·L`

Each factor is read from the field the engine already has, not authored:

- `1 − t_L` is the share of the ray's beam that layer's **foliage** takes out — the scattering density, read from the tap's rgb and never from its wood channel (see the split below).
- `S_L` is the irradiance arriving at that layer **from above**: `sunColor·A_L + ambient`, where `A_L` is the product of the layers over `L`, tapped along the **sun's** ray at `p_L + g·(h_M − h_L)` with `g = sunDir.xy/sunDir.z` — the same per-metre plan travel `uBulkShift` carries on the floor, read in the opposite sense because here it is where the light comes *from*. For four layers that is six extra taps and no new data.
- `ŝ_L = t_L / max(t_L)` is the layer's transmittance normalized to **unit peak**: hue with the brightness divided out, §3.5's idiom and the curtain dye's twin. The chlorophyll spectrum carries the colour and the knob carries the level, which is why a crown reads green-gold at any `sky_scatter`. Taking the *layer's* spectrum rather than one leaf's is also a first-order account of scattered light filtering back out through the layer that made it.

**The energy bound is structural, not a clamp.** `Σ_L (Π_{M<L} t_M)(1 − t_L)` telescopes to `1 − Π_M t_M` exactly, per channel (measured over 200 000 random four-layer stacks: max deviation `2·10⁻¹⁶`). With `ŝ ≤ 1` and `sky_scatter ≤ 1` the whole term is bounded by `sky_scatter·(1 − T)·(sunColor + ambient)`: a crown can never radiate more than the light landing on it, and a stack deep enough to darken has, by the same expression, little light left to scatter. `sky_scatter = 0` leaves `L` exactly zero and adds it to the transmission term, which is exact in IEEE 754 — pure transmission, byte for byte.

**The wood occludes the glow as it occludes the sky.** On an upward ray the trunks and limbs are crossed *before* the crowns, so the analytic occluder's transmittance multiplies the accumulated scatter as well as `T`. That is a first-order statement — scatter born below a limb should not be blocked by it — and it is right in the geometry this view has, where the wood stands under the canopy it silhouettes against.

**The skeleton joins the seen layers.** Leaves must have twigs to hang on, or a crown reads as a cloud floating near a bare limb tip. In the look-up camera the fine wood is stamped into the **layer textures** at bake time as neutral per-channel optical depth, additively, exactly as the leaves are — one more thing in the same texture, read by the same tap.

*Do not bin long wood into the cast layers.* A segment that spans several bands is cut into pieces whose heights are snapped to different layers, and on the cast each piece then shifts by its own layer's `h·g`: a continuous branch becomes a staircase of disjoint shadows (§4.5's recorded dead end, and why the trunk and main limbs are an analytic continuous-height occluder instead). The **seen** view has the same hazard for the same reason — its read `eye.xy + dir.xy·(h_L/dir.z)` is height-dependent too — so the rule is the same and the fix is the same: only wood short enough to sit inside one band goes into a band. Level 0 and 1 are the trunk and main limbs, already drawn analytically as real capsules; **level 2 and above** — sub-branches and twigs — are what the layers take.

**Scattering density is foliage alone, and the channel split is what enforces it.** *An opaque occluder in the scatter channel glows.* The source term reads its density from `1 − t_L` and its hue from `t_L` normalized to unit peak; wood is neutral, so a twig's depth normalizes to a near-**white** hue at near-**full** density, and the term answers with almost the whole illumination. The skeleton then renders as pale filaments **brighter than the sky they stand against** — an occluder outshining what it occludes, and worse the thicker the wood. So the two occluders live in different channels of the same texel:

> layer texel `= (τ_foliage.rgb, τ_wood.a)`;  transmittance `= e^(−τ_rgb)·e^(−τ_a)`;  scatter density and hue `= e^(−τ_rgb)` **only**

The alpha channel is free: the layer textures are `RGBA16F`, their alpha has only ever carried accumulated leaf coverage, and nothing reads it — every tap takes `.rgb`. Claiming it costs one colour mask around the leaf draw (so the leaves stop writing coverage there) and one line in the stamp's fragment shader, which writes `(0,0,0,τ)`: a zero in rgb leaves the foliage depth untouched under the additive blend. The clear stays **unmasked**, because alpha now means something and has to reset.

Wood still appears in the ray's **throughput**, and in the sun's path to each layer — a twig shadows a leaf exactly as another leaf does. It appears nowhere in the emission. That makes the bound structural rather than clamped: wood can only multiply the result by a factor `≤ 1`, so a wood pixel is darker than the same ray without it, always. Measured against a shipped-daylight sky of luminance 198: a bare twig at `branch_tau 2.2` reads 63, at 3.2 reads 31, at 5.0 reads 10 — monotonically darker with thickness, which is the direction an occluder must move. Read the other way it read 216, 217, 217: *brighter* than the sky, and brighter the more opaque the wood. A ray that meets nothing but wood emits exactly zero at every `sky_scatter` up to 1.

**Registration holds by construction, not by tuning.** A terminal segment is stamped into **its own clump's layer**, the very bin the leaves hanging on it went into, so wood and leaves are drawn at the same plan point and read at the same plan point; the residual is zero rather than small. The coherent sway is taken the same way: the stamp receives the **leaf bake's own per-layer factor** `1 + sway_height_gain·(h_L/base − 1)` as a rigid plan translation, instead of the cast path's height-scaled `h/h_ref`, because a twig and its leaves must move together and the leaves move by the layer's rule. *Where two things must stay registered, give them one rule, not two rules that agree today.*


**No receiver.** There is no surface between the canopy and the eye, so the whole receiver subsystem is inert: no `Tt`, no dye, no folds, no seams, no ground albedo, no fold warp, no window. `faithful_canopy` is forced off for one step further than the enclosure's reason — the enclosure has no *single* cast frame, and the sky view has no cast frame at all, because the pre-integration is along the sun's rays and this reads along the eye's. There is nothing for a single tap to be a tap of.

Honest approximations, stated: **the sky is a flat radiance** — one colour across the dome, with no horizon brightening and no sun-adjacent aerosol forward-scatter, so the aureole in frame is the *sampler's* halo and not the atmosphere's; a real gradient wants a sky model the engine does not have, and faking one with a screen-space ramp would be painting. Below the horizon the frame hands off to §4.7's distance haze — one behaviour and one colour shared with the floor path's over-horizon rays, rather than a second sky model for the bottom of the picture. The scatter is **single** scatter: light that bounces twice inside the canopy is not carried, so a very dense crown is darker here than in life, where multiple scattering is most of what fills it. The layer-stamped fine wood is stamped **without** the leaves' twig-hug jitter, and a fine twig near the texel scale of the bake will alias like any other thin feature. Wood occludes only where its closest approach stands **in front of the eye** (a 5–20 cm ramp on the along-ray distance): wood at the eye is not between the eye and the sky, and without that gate an eye standing on a trunk's axis — tree 0 is planted at plan (0,0), so a `view_center` at the origin does exactly that — is shadowed by it in *every* direction, multiplying the whole frame, sky and sun included, by `exp(−τ)`. The gate makes that pose survivable, never composed: *the hammock hangs between trees; do not stand the eye on a trunk.* The wood's silhouette gets **one constant angular soft edge**, standing in for both a pixel's own footprint and the penumbra a silhouette really has against the sun's disk; physically it should be `θ`-soft only where the wood stands against the *source* and knife-sharp against blue sky, and knowing which needs the source's angular coverage at the wood's own position — a second ray march. The eclipse's renormalization is analytic (circle–circle overlap) so the seen field carries exactly the sampler's total energy, but the sampler's own 32-point estimate of the moon's bite differs from the truth by ~10 % in the core:halo split — that gap belongs to the sampler, not to the view. The diffusion radius still divides by `view_extent_m`, which frames nothing here, so those two knobs set a plain screen-space glare radius. And §3.3 is **reaffirmed, not quietly dropped**: *there is no focal plane.* Looking at the canopy instead of its cast changes nothing about that — the camera is still a pinhole, there is no eye depth of field, no bokeh, no near/far blur. Every soft edge in the frame is the source's angular size or the wood's antialiasing, exactly as it is on the floor.

The enclosure's **mesh panels** (§4.9) are windows onto precisely this view — a mesh is not a cast surface but a hole you see the canopy through — so per-panel material and this branch are the two halves of one step.

## 5. Motion

**One wind field, two bands, feeding one spring hierarchy.** Every node in the branch hierarchy is a little transform with a **rest pose** and a **damped spring** pulling it back; children inherit their parent's motion. The wind is an offset *force*, never a thing that drifts the rest pose.

The two motions remembered in the park are not two strengths of one thing — they are **two different mechanisms** living at two ends of the hierarchy.

### 5.1 The coherent band (top of the hierarchy)

A **slow, smooth, directional** wind that loads the big nodes — trunk, major limbs. Because everything hangs off them, the whole canopy shifts together and **the pattern translates nearly intact.**

Three specifics, each a required piece of the model:

- **Breathing / easing.** The wind signal is low-frequency with **attack and decay** — gusts ease in and out, never a square edge.
- **Nonlinear stiffening.** A real branch resists harder the more it bends, so past a point the motion **clips against a ceiling.** This stiffening is what is *felt* as the branch fighting the wind at the peak of a gust.
- **Backlash at the stiffening.** A branch bent hard against its ceiling has stored a lot of elastic energy. When it wins or the gust eases, it does **not** glide home — it **recoils past rest and oscillates back down.** The snap-back is **underdamped**, and the overshoot grows with how hard the branch was pushed into the ceiling. So this band is not *load → relax*; it is **load → clip against stiffening → whip-back → settle.**
- **Exact relaxation.** It returns to a **stored, honest equilibrium** — the original starting point.

**Realizing it (first concrete model).** Before a full branch hierarchy exists, the whole-tree node is collapsed into a **single 1-D spring** along the wind direction, and its displacement is applied as a **near-uniform translation of the canopy plane at bake time** — every leaf shifts by the same vector, so the dapple field slides bodily, which is exactly "translates nearly intact." A `height_gain` optionally lets higher layers ride longer levers and sway a touch more; at zero it is pure translation. The spring is the four specifics above, made literal:

- The wind is a **force**, not a target; the spring's rest is **0**, so relaxation is exact for free.
- **Stiffening** is a restoring force that *diverges at a ceiling* — `restoring ∝ u/(1−u²)` with `u` the sway as a fraction of the ceiling — so no finite gust can push past it, and the resistance climbs as it nears the limit (the "fighting").
- **Backlash** is the same underdamped spring with **asymmetric damping**: damping is cut on the *return* stroke (when velocity opposes displacement), so a branch released from near the ceiling whips back *past* rest and rings down, and the overshoot grows with how hard it was loaded — no separate mechanism.
- **Breathing** is an **asymmetric one-pole envelope** (independent attack and decay constants) on a smooth low-frequency gust signal, so gusts ease in and out rather than switching.

This is deliberately the *reduced* form of the §5 hierarchy (one node, not a tree); it is faithful to the felt behavior and is the seam the real spring graph will later plug into.

**Correction from feel-testing: translation alone is not wind.** A single rigid translation — every leaf shifted by the identical vector — does *not* read as wind, even with correct breathing and backlash. Two things were missing, and both are already in the spec elsewhere; the coherent band must carry them:

- **Leaves change angle as the wind blows.** The wind rocks each leaf, and by §4.5 a tilting leaf's footprint **foreshortens** — so the dapple *changes shape*, not just position, and returns to rest when the gust passes. Without this every blob keeps its exact silhouette and merely slides, which is the tell that betrays it as a translation. The rock has a quasi-static part (the leaf *flags* with the wind, ∝ the signed sway) and a fluttering part whose amplitude grows with wind strength (∝ |sway|), decorrelated in phase per leaf. At zero wind it vanishes, so the rest image is untouched.
- **The sway is not uniform.** Wind "is never only in one axis": clumps (twigs) must flex by **different amounts and in slightly different directions**, so the field shears and gaps morph rather than the whole sheet marching in lockstep. This is precisely the §5.4 *medium band* ("mid-branches & twigs flex differently → gaps morph and rearrange, then recover"), reached here as a per-clump variation on the single coherent sway rather than as its own hierarchy level — leaves are rigid within a clump, clumps differ from one another.

The lesson generalizes: **motion that only moves leaf *positions* will always look like a slide.** A real chunk of "wind" lives in leaf *orientation* driving the footprint (§4.5), and in *decorrelation* between neighbors (§5.2/§5.4). Position is the least of it.

**Now realized: a hierarchy of ROTATIONS, not translations.** The crucial correction. A first hierarchy attempt drove each limb and twig as a 2-D *translation* spring — clumps slid around the plan plane, each toward its own offset. It looked **wrong in an uncanny, almost hallucinatory way**: patches of dapples swimming in different directions with no rigid body behind them, so the eye cannot resolve it as a structure. The reason is physical — **a branch does not translate; it pivots.** A branch is anchored at a joint, has a length and an angle, and the wind *rotates* it about that joint; everything hanging off sweeps an arc and the whole sub-structure turns as one rigid piece. Rotation is the thing the eye reads as "branch." So the band is rebuilt as articulated rotation:

- **Trunk** — the single coherent spring of §5.1 (gust → stiffening → backlash); its sway still **translates** the whole field (the bulk drift). Unchanged.
- **Limbs** — a handful of arms **radiating from the trunk at the canopy centre**, each carrying a **scalar bend angle** on a soft/slow spring and pivoting **about the trunk** — the one joint where every limb meets it. A clump attaches to the limb whose direction points **toward** it, so it hangs off that limb's **far end**: every clump sits outward from the trunk pivot, all on one side, and a limb's bend swings its whole wedge of clumps as a rigid arc. Clumps sharing a limb swing together; neighbouring wedges bend by different amounts, so the field **shears at their seams** and gaps morph. The bend is the **wind's torque about the trunk** (below), plus a slow spatial drift so limbs differ.
- **Twigs** — every clump carries its own faster, stiffer bend angle, and swings about a **stem joint** — a base point offset from the clump *toward the trunk*. On the layer/park path that joint is **synthesised** (`stem_length` back along the clump→trunk radial, its limb's base, with a little per-twig angular spread so they don't lockstep); in **faithful** mode it is the twig's **actual grown base**, shared with the twig's own wood (§4.5), which needs no spread because the grown headings already differ. Bending the twig sweeps the whole clump to one side through an arc; leaves sweep arcs and the clump turns, decorrelated clump-to-clump.

A leaf inherits **twig-swing ∘ limb-swing ∘ trunk-translation**, and — critically — its **own orientation rotates with the branch** (`leaf angle += limb bend + twig bend`), which is what makes its footprint present differently as the branch turns. Because each clump is now a *rigid pivot* rather than a free-floating offset, the motion reads as branches in air instead of the earlier soup. The bend **breathes with the gust** and **rests at zero wind** (still image untouched).

**The stem joint is load-bearing — without it you get a vortex.** An earlier version pivoted each twig about its *own centroid*. Mathematically tidy, but it makes the two halves of a clump shear in opposite directions — a little **in-place spin**, i.e. a vortex — and a field of these reads as unphysical "swirling" the eye latches onto, with no agreement with the wind direction. The cure is to move the pivot **off the clump, onto a stem toward the parent**: now the whole clump (which is small next to the stem) swings the *same* way, a directional sweep, with only gentle rotation on top. Pivot location, not pivot amount, is what separates "branch in wind" from "vortex." (Set the stem length to zero and the swirl returns — the knob *is* the diagnosis.) The faithful path later collected the same property for nothing, by growing it: a real twig's base already sits off the clump toward its parent, so it *is* a stem joint, and unifying the leaf pivot with it bought the wood-registration too (§4.5). Still **shallow** — the whole tree lives in the plan plane, not yet 3-D branches with real length and droop — but the kinematic principle (*wind changes branch angles about real joints; children inherit the rotation*) is now the spec's, and the stem was the change that pushed the **twigs** out of the uncanny valley.

**Then the trunk became real — and the vortex came back one level up.** With the stem joint curing the *twig* swirl, the same failure surfaced at the **limb** level, for the same reason: limbs were scattered plan anchors with clumps bound to the *nearest* one, so a limb's clumps **surrounded** its pivot — opposite sides swept opposite ways, each region spinning in place, a coarser vortex. (This is what reads as *whole planes swirling*.) The cure is the stem-joint principle lifted a level: give the tree a **trunk at the canopy centre**, grow the limbs as **arms** from it, and bind each clump to the limb whose direction points **toward** it — so every clump hangs off the far end, outward from the trunk, never straddling it. All limbs pivot about that **one** joint.

But a shared pivot springs a second trap: if every limb bends by the *same* angle about the same point, the whole canopy rotates rigidly — a field of little vortices traded for **one giant pinwheel**, still a swirl. So a limb's bend is not a uniform lean but the **wind's torque about the trunk**, `∝ cross(limb_dir, wind)`: limbs **broadside** to the wind swing hardest, limbs **end-on** barely move, and the two sides swing in **opposite senses** — every tip travels *downwind*, so the canopy **leans** rather than spins. The earlier "limbs lean downwind together" was the simplification that had to go; orientation-dependent torque is what lets a *shared* pivot read as a tree in wind. The twig `u`-bend now takes the same torque, for the same reason.

> Lesson, restated and stronger: not only does moving *positions* alone look like a slide (above) — moving positions *differently per region* looks like **soup**. The differential motion of a canopy is **rotational**: angles changing at joints, inherited down a tree. Translation is only the trunk's bulk drift; everything below it is rotation — and rotation about a **shared** joint must be driven by **torque** (an orientation-dependent bend), never a uniform angle, or it spins. The vortex is **scale-free**: it returns at every level where children straddle a pivot turned by a single angle, and it dies the same way each time — put the pivot on the parent (so children are one-sided) *and* drive the bend by torque (so a gust leans, not rotates).

**The joints are now real (a grown grove).** The kinematics above originally drove a *fake* skeleton (clustered points around the canopy centre). The canopy is now a grown grove (§4.5): each tree's limbs pivot about **its own** trunk, twigs about real grown stem joints, and the limb/twig torque is taken about that tree's trunk — so a gust leans every tree about its own base, not the frame centre. A leaf inherits its tree's limb-swing ∘ twig-swing ∘ the global trunk drift. The core motion is **2-D yaw** in the ground plane — branches turn about joints.

**Depth, as a second rotational DOF (`sway_pitch`).** Each limb carries a **pitch** angle beside its yaw bend, on its own spring with the **same** stiffness and damping (no new stiffness knobs); `sway_pitch` scales how much of it reaches the cast. The two DOFs are the **same wind force resolved two ways about the same joint**: the drag **perpendicular** to a limb's plan direction has a moment arm about the vertical and **yaws** it (`∝ cross(limb_dir, wind)`, above); the drag **parallel** to it has no such arm — it cannot turn the limb about the trunk at all — so it loads the limb end-on and **pitches** it in its own vertical plane (`∝ dot(live_dir, wind)`). Cross and dot are complementary; between them they spend the whole force. Both resolve against the **balanced mechanical fan** — the same basis the yaw torque uses, deliberately decoupled from the drawn azimuth (§4.5) — so neither DOF can spin the canopy.

**Streamlining is emergent, not a special case.** The pitch is forced by the limb's **live** direction: the mechanical fan *rotated by that limb's current yaw bend*. So the two DOFs act in sequence without being sequenced. A **broadside** limb is all cross and no dot: it takes the yaw torque and swings downwind. That swing turns its live direction **into the wind's line**, the dot grows — and only *then* does the limb pitch flat. It aligns, and then it streamlines, in that order, because the first motion is what creates the second forcing. There is no alignment test anywhere in the code. An **upwind** limb (`dot < 0`) takes negative torque and pitches up.

**What reaches the cast** is the same readout as the first cut, with the pitch angle in place of the yaw angle as its argument: a pitched limb foreshortens the heights of the leaves *and* the wood on it by `fore = clamp(1 − sway_pitch·(1 − cos θ_pitch), 0.1, 1)`, so they cast a **shorter, foreshortened** shadow — the depth dimension the flat yaw lacked. It is **faithful-mode only** (it needs the real per-leaf/per-segment heights; the layer cast has none), applied to leaves and wood by the **same** factor off the **same** per-limb pitch state so the leaf-on-wood registration holds, and **0 = off, byte-identical** — with the knob off the pitch spring never integrates and its state is held at rest, so re-enabling it (or a transition tweening it up) starts from rest rather than snapping. The foreshorten is measured from the limb's **attach height** — the height at which it meets its trunk — not from the ground: `h' = attachH + (h − attachH)·fore`. At the join `h' = attachH` exactly, so a pitching limb cannot part from the trunk axis (which does not pitch): the join is closed **by construction**, not by tuning. The sign is right for a droop tip hanging *below* its attach too — rotating a limb down about its joint moves every point *toward* the attach plane.

**One approximation, recorded.** `1 − cos` is **even**, so an upwind limb pitched *up* foreshortens slightly instead of **lifting** its cast. The named next refinement is a **signed** response — which needs each limb's **rest elevation** plumbed into the motion state, since up and down are only meaningful relative to the elevation the limb already sits at. Deliberately not done here: the up case is the smaller effect, and the shipped first cut had the same evenness, so it is a carried-over approximation, not a new one.

*(Do not drive the foreshorten from the yaw bend: a limb turning sideways in plan does not by itself lose elevation — depth needs its own DOF, and the live-direction forcing above is what makes streamlining mechanical rather than implied. And do not scale heights from the ground: that leaves the trunk unscaled and opens a hairline gap at the limb↔trunk join under a low sun — the attach-height anchor closes it.)* Off by default; tune by eye. A true articulated depth-lean of the whole skeleton — every branch order pitching, not just the limb — remains the deeper next step.

**Now realized: the forcing is BROADBAND, with weather and crosswind.** The first model drove the spring with a single near-sinusoidal gust (one `gust_frequency` plus an attack/decay envelope). It read as *mechanical and one-directional* — and the reason is physical: real wind is **broadband**, its energy spread across every timescale at once (a minute-long surge carrying second-scale gusts carrying sub-second flutter), falling off with a characteristic **Kolmogorov −5/3 power spectrum** in the gust range. A single frequency puts all its energy at one period, so the eye locks onto it and reads a machine. The cure is to drive the spring with a **fractal sum of octaves over time** — frame-stable noise-of-time, so still no sampling RNG (§4.4): the per-octave amplitude gain `G = 2^(−H)` sets the spectral slope, with `H = 1/3` the physically-correct wind value (between "pink" `H=0`, choppy/nervous, and "brown" `H=0.5`, smooth/sluggish). `H` *is* the **character** of the wind.

Three controls fall out, each a real meteorological quantity:

- **strength** — the mean force ("how much").
- **gustiness** = **turbulence intensity** `σ/U` (≈0.1 calm … 0.2 open country … higher under a canopy) — "how alive." It scales the fluctuation against the mean; at high gustiness the deep lulls drop the force below the mean lean, so the spring recoils **back through rest** — the felt "comes back," now arising from the forcing itself, not only the backlash.
- **gust rate** = the lowest-octave frequency ("how frequent"); faster octaves and flutter layer on top automatically. *(This is the old `gust_frequency`, reinterpreted as the base rate of the broadband signal.)*

And the motion is no longer 1-D. A **lateral (crosswind) channel** — a second, decorrelated broadband stream on its own spring, perpendicular to the wind — runs alongside the longitudinal one (turbulence has no preferred sign across-wind, so it is genuinely bidirectional). The two compose into a 2-D sway, so the bulk drift **wanders** rather than tracking a dead-straight downwind line. *(This is the trunk-level seat of the §5.4 medium-band decorrelation; the limb/twig hierarchy adds the rest.)*

**Wind "patterns" — a few broadband characters.** Rather than expose `H`, octave count, asymmetry and burstiness as raw knobs, they are bundled into a small named set — **steady · gusty · squally · choppy · lazy** — each a pre-tuned *character* that all read the **same** shared knobs (strength, gustiness, rate, direction, weather), shaped differently inside (spectral slope, detail depth, mean-lean fraction, crosswind fraction, and a waveshaper that spikes peaks and deepens lulls for clustered/intermittent gusts). The gust-edge **asymmetry** (rise sharper than decay, ~1.35×, validated in the field) survives as the existing attack/decay envelope, now slewing the **longitudinal** broadband force (the lean channel); the crosswind channel is zero-mean and left un-slewed, so its bidirectional gusts aren't biased. A pattern is selected by name (`wind_pattern`) — the same minimal surface the §9 looks reference; it can later be promoted to a per-pattern generator function without changing that surface.

**Weather — the slowest band.** Above the gusts sits a **slow, self-evolving weather state**: low-frequency noise-of-time (again deterministic, so reproducible and frame-stable — a stochastic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck mean-reverting walk would do the same job but break the no-RNG rule) that drifts the **overall strength** (calm spells and gusty spells coming and going over minutes) and slowly **veers the direction**. `weather_variability` sets how much the day evolves on its own (0 = static, so existing looks are untouched) and `weather_speed` how fast. This is the difference between a calm afternoon and a gusty one — and it changes, unbidden, which way the wind leans.

The lesson, restated and stronger: **the first primitive was wrong, not just mis-tuned.** Wind is not a frequency with an amplitude; it is a *spectrum across timescales*, with a mean that itself drifts. Strength / gustiness / rate / pattern / weather are that spectrum made controllable — the broadband signal subsumes the single gust, and the deep lulls + crosswind retire the "leans downwind and never comes back" tell on their own.

### 5.2 The incoherent band (bottom of the hierarchy)

A **fast, spatially incoherent** forcing — "faint, in all directions" — that kicks **individual leaves and petioles** slightly differently, with almost no correlation between neighbors. That **decorrelation is the entire difference** from the coherent band: there, neighbors move together and shape is preserved; here, neighbors move independently, so gaps **reorganize** rather than translate.

Its visible effect scales from a faint **glisten** all the way up to full **caustic-like gap reorganization** — and what sets the scale is foliage density (§5.3).

**The caustics reference is physics, not just a vibe, and belongs in the model.** Pool caustics are bright features that move by **merging, splitting, and collapsing** rather than sliding. What reorganizes here is the same *visual grammar*: small gaps drifting in and out of the pinhole regime, neighboring sun-images overlapping and separating, brightness gathering and dispersing as apertures breathe. It is **not** literal optical focusing the way water is — but the felt behavior (smooth birth, merge, and collapse of bright shapes) is shared, and it tells an implementer what the second motion should *feel* like in a way no equation does.

**Driving it directly (a single continuous phase).** The band can be exercised without any wind by exposing one continuous **drift phase** φ. Each leaf is given a fixed rest position and a small, bounded **orbit** — amplitude, orientation, and starting phase all *decorrelated* between neighbors — and advancing φ walks every leaf around its own orbit. Because the orbits are bounded and share a rate, the canopy is **periodic in φ and returns exactly to its starting configuration each cycle** — this is the remembered "the gaps change, then go back to what they were." The neighbor-decorrelation is, again, the whole point: it makes gaps *reorganize* (merge, split, collapse) rather than translate. The one hard requirement is **continuity of the driver**: φ and φ+ε must differ only infinitesimally, so the eye reads a *morph*, not a cut. Any control that instead reshuffles a random *set* — most obviously changing an integer leaf **count** — steps discontinuously and reads as a jump-cut, which is exactly why density is the wrong knob for this. When the incoherent wind band (§5.4) is built, it simply *becomes* the driver of φ; exposing φ as a manual slider is the same mechanism with time replaced by a hand.

### 5.3 Density is a first-class knob

Whether independent leaf motion **restructures** the gaps or merely **twinkles** them is set by foliage density:

- **Low density** (the early-spring condition — sparse, immature leaves): individual leaves still matter; moving one opens or closes a real gap → the **caustic-like reorganization** is possible. *Low density is the enabling condition for the second motion, and the spec should say so plainly.*
- **High density** (summer canopy): every gap is backed by ten more leaves; the topology is saturated; the same flutter barely changes the cast pattern → flutter only **twinkles.**

Note that density is a *count*, and a count is a poor axis to *sweep*: nudging it regenerates a different random set, so the pattern jumps rather than morphs (see §5.2). Two non-physical harness measures make density pleasant to explore without changing the model: generate **each clump from its own stable seed** (so changing one knob never reshuffles unrelated clumps), and **fade the fractional marginal leaf in by coverage** (so crossing an integer count is continuous rather than a pop). These are about smoothing the *parameter space*, not the physics; the actual gap *reorganization* is the drift phase of §5.2, not density.

### 5.4 The unifying mapping

The three motions are the **same structure seen at three timescales**, mapped onto three levels of the hierarchy:

| Motion seen on the ground | Hierarchy level | Timescale |
|---|---|---|
| Wind **strengthens, calms, and veers** | (weather) modulates the whole coherent band | very slow (minutes) |
| Pattern **translates** intact | whole tree / major limbs sway together | slow |
| Gaps **morph and rearrange**, then recover | mid-branches & twigs flex differently from one another | medium |
| Pattern **glistens** | each leaf trembles on its own petiole | fast |

The bands **coexist and sum**, and the coherent band is itself **broadband** — not one slow rate but a fractal spread of them (§5.1), with a weather layer drifting its strength and direction on top. On the spring afternoon, a gentle directional breeze and a fine all-directions shimmer were both present, in shifting proportion, moment to moment. **That proportion, the wind's character (pattern + gustiness), and the foliage density are most of the expressive surface of the whole piece.**

## 6. Implementation target

- **Platform:** WebGL / HTML5, a full-screen quad with the heavy lifting in a fragment shader.
- **Canopy** lives as objects (CPU or compute), carrying rest pose and spring state, **re-baked to the depth-layer transmittance textures once per frame.**
- **Transport** is the shift-multiply-sum over **~16–48 source samples**, read in the fragment shader; target 60 fps.
- **Transport is ONE master source, compiled per camera.** The receiver question — *what did the light land on* — has four answers: the floor, the standing cloth, the enclosure stood around the viewer, and the retina looking up (§4.7/§4.9). They are not four effects to blend, they are four programs. And a camera is a **mode**: it can only change through a structural rebuild, never inside a frame, which makes it a **compile-time selection** rather than a runtime branch. So transport lives as named snippets — camera blocks, occluder tiers, materials, the shared Look — each carrying its own uniform declarations, and a builder emits, for one camera, exactly the ones that camera runs. The branches are not rewritten; they **disappear by omission**, so within a variant every emitted line is the line the single shader ran, and the two flags that used to select them do not survive into any variant at all: the branch they chose *is* the build key. What this buys is **headroom, not speed** — the four-in-one shader declared 246 vec4 rows of uniforms against WebGL2's guaranteed 224, and the variants stand at 213 (floor), 222 (cloth), 229 (enclosure) and 158 (sky). Each program is compiled on first use of its camera and kept for the engine's life, so the one compile a camera ever costs lands inside the rebuild a mode flip already hides under the transition bloom (§9). *(What it does not yet buy: the woody occluder's segment table is 128 of those rows by itself and every camera reads it, which is why the enclosure is still over the guaranteed minimum. Getting that table off the uniform budget is the next question, not this one.)* The direction this opens is that cameras and materials stop being names inside a builder and become **registry entries** — a receiver contributing its own snippets, uniforms and uploads — which is what turns adding a receiver into an addition rather than an edit.
- **The spring graph** lives in the canopy objects; the two wind bands drive it.
- **Render linear HDR, tone-map last.**
- **One shared engine, its looks, and its consumers.** The whole renderer is a standalone **ES module** — `komorebi.js`, `export { create, DEFAULTS, MAX_LAYERS, MAX_SAMPLES, DEG }` (plus the transition key-sets `MORPH_KEYS`/`CANOPY_KEYS`/`TOPO_KEYS`, exported only so a test can assert every knob is classified, §9) — where `create(canvas, { params })` returns a handle (`apply(scope)`, `setParams`, `transitionTo(params, {duration, onEnd})` for a cloud-bloom crossfade between looks (§9), `setPaused`, `dispose()` (stop the loop and free every GL object + the context — so a disposable second instance can be reclaimed), live `params`/`perf`/`motion`/`src`/`trans`, an `onFrame` hook). It runs its own rAF loop on whatever canvas it's handed and carries no UI. The **built-in looks are a separate module** — `presets.js`, `export const PRESETS`, each look merged over the engine's `DEFAULTS` — because the presets are *data*, not renderer (§9), and keeping the engine free of them lets it stay a pure renderer. Two pages import both directly: the **editor** (`index.html`, a thin shell that loads `editor.js` — the dev panel, HUD, insets, preset management, sun-drag, scene-stepping, and the §9 auto-profiler UI; its pure sub-concerns are split into siblings `presets-store.js` (★-look localStorage I/O) and `profiler.js` (the auto-profiler's cost taxonomy + variant algorithm), each unit-tested under `bun test`) and the reference **player** (`player.html` — a minimal viewer-only consumer: a full-bleed canvas cycling through presets, no UI). Because they use native ES-module `import`, both must be **served over http(s)**, not opened off the filesystem. The eljojo.net homepage embeds the player pattern too, but as a no-build classic-`<script>` site, so it loads a bundled `window.Komorebi` global instead (the `komorebi.global.js` deploy shim, §9). `create()` throws on missing WebGL2/float targets so a consumer can degrade to a static page. In the editor build only, the handle also carries a small `profiler` (GPU timer-query instrumentation + an offscreen stress burst) and a motion mirror, both used by the §9 auto-profiler and both dead-stripped from the player; the general `setPaused`/`dispose()` ship in both, so the profiler's A/B wipe can spin up a second engine per comparison and fully reclaim it on close. The engine ships as a pure renderer.

## 9. Harness notes (non-physical)

These belong to the authoring tool, not the model, but they shape how the model is *explored* and so are worth recording.

- **Presets.** The full parameter set is saveable to the browser's local storage by name, and any state can be exported/imported as JSON for sharing across machines. A set of looks ships built in — all grown groves, spanning mornings, afternoons (some with hazier / lower-sun variants) and a few set pieces, such as a single-tree *eclipse* whose gaps image a partially-eclipsed sun (§3.4 — every dapple a matching crescent), and a *prism* whose dapples fringe red-and-blue from leaf-edge diffraction (§3.6). The **receiver** looks (§4.9) ship there too — curtain scenes on the standing cloth, the tent enclosure seen from inside, and the *canopy* series seen from below through the sky view — one physics across every surface the light lands on. **They live in their own module, `presets.js` (`export const PRESETS`), split out of `komorebi.js` so the engine carries no look data; each look is merged over the engine's `DEFAULTS`, and their definition order in that file *is* the dropdown / arrow-stepping order — there is no separate order list.** The editor boots into one of them (*afternoon 7*, auto-quality on); every other look is the user's own, saved (★) to local storage. The bare neutral config (`DEFAULTS`) is the merge base; it stays in the engine (exported, imported by `presets.js`) but is not itself a preset. **A look saved yesterday must still load tomorrow, so a renamed knob keeps its old spelling forever**: the engine owns a single alias map (`LEGACY_KEYS` in `komorebi.js`) applied at every door external params come through — `create`, `setParams`, `transitionTo` and the ★ store — translating retired names to current ones (a current name already present wins); nothing the engine writes back out ever uses an old one.
- **Stable per-clump seeding & fractional-leaf fade.** As in §5.3, so the parameter space is smooth to sweep.
- **Manual drift phase.** The §5.2 mechanism, exposed as a slider (`drift_phase`) with an `auto` option that advances it over time — a preview of the incoherent band before any real wind field exists.
- **Auto-quality (debug toggle).** A checkbox — **on** in the default scene (*afternoon 7*), though the underlying param defaults off — that watches frame rate and eases toward the highest quality that still holds ~60 fps. It spends the *cheapest* quality first — render resolution (nearly free for so soft a piece), then source-sample count **and bake resolution** together below the knee (the bake lever reaches the co-dominant bake pass, §9) — and leaves the physical and artistic params untouched. *(In **`faithful_canopy`** mode this order flips: transport is a single tap so resolution buys almost nothing, while the per-sample geometry bake — linear in the sample count — is the whole cost, so the governor trims **samples first across the full range** there. The `park 1` standing look ships with auto-quality **on** because faithful is the heaviest path and, under steady wind, the adaptive-fps saver can't idle it.)* It chases a smooth 60 — dropping quality (debounced against lone spikes) whenever frames slip below it — and is deliberately **reluctant to climb back**: each forced downsize doubles the wait before it will probe upward again, so it ratchets to a stable level and parks there rather than hunting. Full quality is re-probed only on an explicit re-tune (toggling it, or loading a preset).
- **Preset auto-profiler (debug tool).** An editor-only profiling mode (a **profile** button in the Debug disclosure) that measures where a preset spends its frame budget and authors lighter variants from it. Because the loop is vsync-locked, naive fps reads flat on capable hardware, so per-pass cost is measured with **GPU timer queries** (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`) averaged over many **real on-screen frames** — the actual transport/bake passes under real conditions — at max quality with the auto-scaler held off. (An offscreen stress-burst was tried and abandoned: rendering a pass N× back-to-back measures idealised GPU *throughput* the driver can collapse, not a real frame, so it wildly over-reported spare capacity — it survives only as a coarse fallback where timer queries are absent.) The honest whole-frame number sits beside the per-pass split: the **live frame rate at full quality** — under 60 means the real frame is over budget, the very thing that makes auto-quality trim, which a synthetic "headroom ×" had obscured. The measurement is split **transport vs bake**, which is the point: `auto_quality` historically trimmed only the transport target (resolution, then samples) and the bake pass was invisible to it — so on the windy looks that ship, where bake is co-dominant, a capable machine could sit at 60 fps and still run hot. It now also trims **bake resolution** below the knee (snapped, reallocating the layer textures only at a level boundary), finally reaching that co-dominant pass — but the split still matters, since the two passes trim on different levers. The tool ablates each cost axis to show its share, classified by the cost audit: **safe** (sample count, texture resolution, layer count — low visual risk), **risky** (foliage density — changes the gap structure, and `faithful_canopy` — mode fidelity itself, the largest quality fork, whose cut is the layer-path fallback: it joins the degradation ladder arbitrated on the A/B wipe like every risky cut, never trimmed silently by the runtime governor), **style** (leaf-edge diffraction, and the receiver's lateral-diffusion glow — deliberate looks, measured for their price, never auto-cut), and **tune** (the performance optimizations — decoupled bake resolution and adaptive frame-rate, both detailed below — measured and auto-proposed like the safe cuts; both now ship **on by default** as a deliberate performance baseline, trading a little fidelity for speed). The whole session runs at **max resolution with the auto-scaler held off**, so every figure is the cost of a *stylistic* decision at full quality (on a weak device the live canvas may already be downscaled, which would otherwise understate it); render resolution itself is deliberately **not** an axis — it is `auto_quality`'s runtime lever, not something the author sets, and the lite variants exist precisely to lower the *static* cost so that scaler has to engage **less**. It then proposes a few progressively-lighter variants — greedy over the *measured* per-axis cost, so an axis is judged on this look rather than a fixed order — and presents them as a **draggable wipe**: a second engine instance on its own stacked canvas, its wind **mirrored exactly** from the main look (`setMotionSource` copies the live motion + spring state each frame — sound because the variants never change the grove skeleton, so the spring arrays line up 1:1), so the gust *and* the flutter match across the seam and only the quality differs — and each engine's live fps is shown either side of the wipe, so on weak hardware you watch the lighter variant hold the refresh while the full look drops. That second engine is created per comparison and **disposed** on close, so the editor holds no second GL context when you're not comparing. The author picks one visually and saves it through the normal ★ preset flow (e.g. *afternoon 7 · lite*) — graceful degradation developed on a fast machine and human-verified, reaching the half of the frame the runtime governor can't. The engine gains only EDITOR-gated hooks (`eng.profiler` measurement + the motion mirror) plus the general `setPaused`/`dispose()`; the axis taxonomy + variant composition live in a pure, unit-tested module (`profiler.js`), the look persistence in `presets-store.js`, and the UI/orchestration in `editor.js` — the engine stays a pure renderer.
- **The quality-rung contract.** A quality *upgrade* never lands as a bare improvement; it lands as a **rung** on a ladder, and owes four things:
  1. a **gate parameter** whose off state is **byte-identical** to the look before it;
  2. a declared **cheaper rung it degrades to** — the next step down, ultimately *off*;
  3. a **profiler AXES entry** whenever it carries measurable per-frame cost, classified by the cost audit above (*safe* / *risky* / *style* / *tune*);
  4. an explicit statement of **who may cross it** — the runtime governor on its own, or only an authored `· lite` variant arbitrated on the A/B wipe.

  `faithful_canopy` is the exemplar: gated (off = the layer fast path, park byte-identical), its fallback rung *is* that layer path, classified **risky**, and crossable **only** by a human-verified lite look — the governor never trims it silently. Motion-model **corrections** are not rungs and don't owe the checklist: broadband wind, the rotation hierarchy, the twig-pivot unification (§4.5) change how the canopy *moves* while leaving the rest image untouched. The contract governs image/cost upgrades, not fixes to the model.
- **The pixel harness (`test-gl/`).** The byte-identical gate discipline is executable, not rhetorical: a headless-Chromium runner (`nix run .#pixels`) renders every shipped look in a real WebGL2 context and writes a PNG per preset, so a look is reviewable as an artifact without a GPU session — by a person or by an agent. Three suites run over those renders: **smoke** (every preset creates, renders, and produces a non-degenerate frame with no GL errors), **gate invariants** (a gated feature's off state is byte-identical *in pixels* — a floor look ignores the entire fabric family, a zero gate ignores its geometry), and **determinism** (two independently created engines under frozen motion produce byte-equal frames — §4.4's no-sampling-RNG rule, measured rather than promised). Captures are byte-stable under frozen motion, which is what lets *equality* be the assertion instead of a tolerance. Playwright's Chromium sits deliberately outside nix (it pins its own build; one `npm install` + `npx playwright install chromium` per machine); the PNGs and `node_modules/` stay untracked — renders are evidence, not source.
- **The profiler is two-way — budget, not just trim.** Beside the per-pass cost and the live full-quality frame rate, the breakdown shows the **spare budget**: one 60 fps frame (16.67 ms) minus the measured cost, from the same honest in-situ timing (not the retired "headroom ×"). When there's spare, the tool offers a single **improve** proposal — the highest-value quality upgrades (more samples, then sharper textures, then an extra depth layer) that *fit* the spare budget without breaking 60, or **nothing** if none fit (no padded recommendation). So the same tool both lightens a heavy look and spends headroom on a light one; both directions land on the same A/B wipe and ★save flow. (`proposeImprove` joins `proposeVariants` in the pure, unit-tested `profiler.js`.)
- **The Tune panel + its two optimizations.** An editor panel section gathers the performance-vs-quality controls that are set per *machine*, not per *look* — sample count, texture **and** bake resolution, depth-layer count, the auto-quality governor, the adaptive-fps saver — out of the artistic sections (foliage density, which varies per look and reshapes the gaps, stays in Canopy). The project ships with both tune optimizations **on by default** as a deliberate performance baseline; either returns to full fidelity per look (bake res → *follow*, adaptive off):
  - **Decoupled bake resolution** (`bake_resolution`, default **1536**; 0 = follow texture res). Sizes the per-frame bake — and the layer textures transport samples — independently of the sample-texture resolution. It ships at 1536 (cheaper than a full 2048 bake) and is now also the lever `auto_quality` trims below the knee (snapped to 256 so the layer textures only reallocate at a level boundary), so the runtime governor finally reaches the **co-dominant bake** on the windy looks. The cost is a softening of the sharpest sub-leaf gaps (the layer texture is `LINEAR`, no new aliasing) — an author axis and an `auto_quality` lever; set it to *follow* for a full-res bake.
  - **Adaptive frame-rate** (`adaptive_motion`, `adaptive_idle_fps`). While the canopy is nearly still — a deep wind lull, or a slow-drift-only look — the heavy bake + transport run at the idle rate into an offscreen target and the rest of the frames re-present it with a byte-identical blit; full rate returns the instant the wind picks up (hysteresis on a motion-magnitude signal driven by the springs, not by slow drift). The direct-to-screen path is untouched when off. The trade is visible stepping on the slow shimmer for a large idle/power saving; the profiler estimates its win as a frame-skip fraction sampled over the measure window (≈0 on a genuinely windy look, high on a calm one).
- **Dev panel hidden by default.** The whole authoring panel is hidden on load; press **D** to reveal it (**F** toggles browser fullscreen). The visualization stands alone until then. **On a touch screen there is no keyboard, so the panel is gesture-driven** — and, unlike the desktop, it does *not* auto-arrive after "feel": a phone opens straight into the full-bleed art with nothing sliding over it (a panel covering a third of a phone screen is the enemy of "feel it first"). A **double-tap** then toggles the panel in and out — the touch equivalent of **D**, persistent. And a **press-and-hold** *peeks* it away: while the finger is down the panel slides off-screen so you can glance at the dapples underneath, then slides back on release. The peek is a momentary hide that leaves the panel's shown/hidden state intact (it never sets `hidden`, only the slide transform), so it reads as "lift the panel to look, drop it back" rather than a dismiss; with the panel already hidden a hold does nothing, there being nothing in the way. The two gestures are deliberately split — **hold to look, double-tap to commit** — and both are guarded so a swipe (presets / time-of-day, above) still wins once the finger travels past the deadzone. Within each subsystem section the knobs are ordered **basic-first**: the handful actually authored per-look lead, and the set-and-forget mechanics (the cloud's halo/samples/eclipse, the canopy's fine leaf-geometry, the wind's spring response) fold into a collapsed *advanced* disclosure; the two least-touched sections (leaf drift, debug) collapse whole. The split was set from a survey of which knobs actually vary across the shipped presets.
- **HUD — signal over echo.** The corner HUD (shown only while the dev panel is open) deliberately carries *only what is live and computed and can't be read off a slider sitting in the panel beside it* — never an echo of a control. So it is two things. A **derived physics readout**: `blur h·θ`, the §3.2 softness scale (canopy height × the sun's angular size), the literal "how soft are the dapples" that no single slider shows. And a **live wind score**: one number that *breathes with the gusts* — the instantaneous broadband forcing, with the base strength and the slow weather spell already folded in — beside a character word read off **effective strength** (the one wind axis the presets actually vary; pattern and gustiness are fixed across every look, so a word off them would be a constant). Performance is treated as a **warning, not a gauge**: fps is hidden until it slips under 60 (shown below 55), and the auto-quality resolution scale shows only while it is compensating below full — so a nominal frame is a single quiet line and the HUD speaks up only when something diverges. The earlier HUD printed fps, tap counts, and echoes of the cloud/sun/wind sliders every frame; the rethink was purely raising signal-to-noise.
- **Per-knob tooltips.** Every control and section heading has a hover tooltip that leads with a plain "this does that" and then builds up to the why/physics — meant to help a newcomer form a mental model of what each knob represents and how it changes the cast pattern, not to be exhaustive.
- **Tree-grove preview (3D).** Press **T** for an inset that shows the *actual* grown grove the dapples come from — the branch skeleton (`hier.segments`) and a blob per leaf — count `leaves_per_cluster × foliage_density`, drawn as a light translucent haze (each leaf's opacity eased down as the count climbs) so the density reads as coverage; each tree's foliage takes a slightly different green so overlapping crowns stay legible, and the branch skeleton (tapered quads — thick trunk to thin twig) is drawn solid behind and again faintly over the haze so it ghosts through — in a slowly-turning 3/4 view, swaying with the live wind (a height-weighted downwind lean + the trunk drift), with a wind-direction arrow and ground grid. CPU-projected into a scissored corner, so it's free unless shown. It makes the otherwise-invisible structure (and the wind model) legible; the sway is an approximation of the bake's per-joint motion, not a re-derivation of it.
- **Scene transitions (VJ).** The **← / →** (and **↑ / ↓**) arrow keys step to the previous/next preset — through the same built-in + saved list the dropdown shows, wrapping at the ends — each a smooth transition rather than a cut. A transition retains as much as it honestly can, falling through **three tiers** keyed on *what actually differs* between the two looks:
  1. **Live morph.** The continuous look (sun, exposure, cloud, wind, colour grade, layer heights) **interpolates** from the current live params to the target with an ease-in-out; angles take the short way round. Read live or rebuilt cheaply, so it just tweens.
  2. **Grove morph.** When the two looks share the same **branching topology** — identical `seed`, `branch_levels`/`children`, `limb_count`, `layer_count` — the grove's RNG draws line up, so interpolating the continuous *canopy* knobs **deforms the very same grove** with **no bloom**: `branch_angle`/`length_ratio`/`pitch` fan the branches; leaf size/aspect/density and `trans_rgb` resize and recolour; `canopy_extent` rescales the baked world (it only needs a regrow + re-bake, not a texture realloc, so it morphs rather than dissolving). **`tree_count` morphs too**, treated as a *continuous* value — `floor(count)` full trees plus a marginal tree faded in by the fraction — so `afternoon 5→6` **grows the 6th tree in** (the others re-spacing and shrinking a touch to make room) instead of popping a new layout. Trees are appended at the *end* of the grove's RNG stream, so a fractional count only ever adds/removes the **last** tree; the existing ones keep their structure — which is why integer counts render byte-identically to before (no preset's look shifts). This is a true morph, not a dissolve; it costs a per-frame regrow, so it runs only while the **peak** grove (the busier, more-trees end) is under a leaf-count budget (`CANOPY_MORPH_MAX`) and otherwise falls back to tier 3. The **in-flight wind sway is preserved across the regrow** — the spring state's common *prefix* carries over, and a newly-grown tree starts at rest — so the breeze doesn't reset; this also fixes editor canopy-slider tweaks resetting the sway mid-gust. (One honest wrinkle: each frame re-bins twigs into depth layers by grown height, so a knob that shifts heights — `branch_pitch`/`angle` — can occasionally flip a single cluster between blur tiers mid-morph; negligible at the 2–3 layers these looks use, so the binning is left live rather than frozen.)
  3. **Cloud dissolve.** Only a genuine **branching change** — a different `branch_levels`/`children`, `limb_count`, `layer_count`, `seed`/`tex_resolution`, a grove too big to morph live (a large `tree_count` jump like `5→16` blows the budget), **or a scene-MODE flip (`faithful_canopy` / `standing_scene` / `receiver`)** — can't interpolate leaf-for-leaf. It is **swapped once at the transition's midpoint, hidden under a transient bloom**: the source is widened toward overcast (the §3.4 cloud knob, pushed up and back) so the field goes soft and low-contrast exactly when the grove regrows. So a big jump still dissolves through a passing cloud; but `afternoon 5→6` grows its extra tree in and `afternoon 4→5` (only `branch_angle`) morphs its branches — both under no cloud. *(The mode flags are their own class — `MODE_KEYS`, distinct from the inert runtime flags below — because flipping one is not just a snap: `faithful_canopy` reallocates the faith texture and switches the bake path, `standing_scene` resizes the crown, `receiver` swaps the whole camera mapping (floor ray-cast ↔ head-on cloth map ↔ enclosure ray-cast), re-aims the faithful cast frame at the cloth, and at `receiver = 2` forces the layer tier — which reallocates the layer textures and re-packs the grove's per-mode cluster data — so the swap must run the full rebuild under the bloom, not assign the flag with stale geometry/textures still in place.)*

  Runtime flags (`auto_quality`, the debug toggles) differing never trigger a bloom — they just settle at the end. *(One faithful-mode wrinkle the live morph must honour: because the faithful cast is **sun-projected** — its cast frame and per-leaf throw are functions of `sun_elevation`/`azimuth` — a still-air time-of-day crossfade between two faithful looks re-bakes the faith texture each frame even with no wind; otherwise the dapple would freeze at the start sun-angle for the whole morph. The layer path needs no such re-bake — transport reprojects every frame from `uProj`/`uBulkShift`.)* The whole thing is one engine entry point, `transitionTo(params, {duration, onEnd})`; the arrow keys are only its first driver (a planned MIDI/event layer will call the same method). The duration is an editor knob, not stored in a look; arrow keys are ignored while a panel control is focused (so they still nudge a slider). Tiers 2–3 reuse the grove growth (§4.5) and the breathing-cloud (§3.4) as a *transition between* looks rather than a state *within* one — modelled, not a painted dissolve.
- **Time of day (sun input).** An editor-only alternative to the raw `sun_elevation`/`sun_azimuth` sliders: a `time_of_day` + `latitude` pair that computes the sun's position for a **spring day** (equinox, declination ≈ 0) — hour angle 15°/hour from solar noon, `elevation = asin(cos lat · cos H)`, azimuth sweeping east→south→west — and writes elevation/azimuth. Latitude caps the noon sun (`90°−lat`). It's a one-way input helper (the angle params remain the source of truth, so presets store the angles, not the time). On a **touch** screen the canvas is swiped rather than drag-positioned: a **horizontal** swipe scrubs `time_of_day` live (the sun arcs across the day) and a **vertical** swipe steps presets (up = next).
- **Desktop camera navigation.** On the desktop the canvas drag defaults to **orbiting the camera** — drag ↔ to spin (`view_yaw_deg`), drag ↕ to tilt (`view_pitch_deg`) — and the **mouse wheel zooms** (`view_extent_m`), so you can move around and make sense of the structure (especially the newly-visible branches). The old **sun-drag** (drag to position the sun in 2-D) is now a toggle in the Look section — a cute novelty kept for playing around, off by default since most editing wants the camera. *(The viewer-only player keeps no input; sun-drag there is a possible future touch for the ambient embeds.)*
- **Page-background player.** The engine doubles as an ambient background, viewer-only (no panel, no input): a fixed full-bleed canvas, optionally with page content on top. The repo ships a minimal reference, `player.html`; the eljojo.net homepage is a real-world embedding of the same pattern. It **rotates through a short cycle of presets** (currently `morning 2` → `afternoon 5b` → `morning 3`), holding each ~30 s and then **cross-fading to the next with `transitionTo`** (the same §9 scene-transition engine the editor's arrow keys drive) — so the background quietly drifts through times of day on its own. It degrades to a static page if WebGL2 is unavailable (the whole thing is wrapped in a `try`, so the page stays fully usable without it). The editor (`index.html`) and the player (`player.html`) share the one engine (`komorebi.js`); see §6.
- **Modules, dev server, and the deploy bundle (bun).** The source is plain ES modules — `komorebi.js` (engine) + `presets.js` (looks), with the editor split across `editor.js` (the authoring shell loaded by the thin `index.html`), `presets-store.js` (★-look persistence), and `profiler.js` (the auto-profiler's pure logic); the player imports only the engine + looks. There is **no build step in the dev loop**: the pages load the raw modules, so a save is live on the next reload. `nix run .#dev` runs a small bun static server (`dev-server.js`) that serves the repo over http (ES modules can't load from `file://`) and **live-reloads** every open tab on any `.js`/`.html`/`.css` change. The one thing that *is* built is the **deploy artifact** for no-build external embeds (the eljojo.net homepage, a classic-`<script>` static site): `nix run .#build` has bun bundle the `komorebi.global.js` shim — which imports the engine + presets and assigns `window.Komorebi` — into `dist/komorebi.player.min.js`, an IIFE global with the editor-only debug overlays **dead-stripped** (`--define KOMOREBI_EDITOR=false` const-folds the `EDITOR` flag, the same effect the old terser+`sed` pass had). So the library is *authored* as ESM (clean to develop, and importable by anyone with a bundler) but also *shipped* as a drop-in global (the lowest-friction form for pasting onto a static page) — the standard library split. The editor's own deploy (komorebi.eljojo.net, GitHub Pages) needs no bundle at all: it serves the raw modules straight off Pages.
